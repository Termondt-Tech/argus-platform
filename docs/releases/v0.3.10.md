# v0.3.10 — Albireo

*TAGGED. (This heading read "DRAFT. Not yet tagged." until 2026-09-10.) Named per the Argus's Eyes convention
(`docs/releases/README.md`) — β Cygni, mapped in Bayer's *Uranometria* (1603) and
medieval cartography as *Oculus Gallinae*, the eye of the Swan. Astronomy's
premier resolvable double star: to an unassisted or blurred view it presents as
one ambiguous point, and only under clear scrutiny does it cleanly split into two
distinct, contrasting stars. Fits a release that is exactly that split, twice —
a broker relabel that made two real fills look like one ambiguous, inconsistent
record until validation moved to cash sign and strike; and two concurrent
handshakes that read as one blurred stall on the event loop until they moved to
a thread where each is its own, resolved cost. Proposed by Richard directly.
Recorded in the Spent table and Notes index below.*

Requires an image rebuild — `occ.py`, `shadow/marker.py`, `shadow/outcome.py`,
`tools/mark.py`, `account/port.py`, `account/stream.py`, `account/fake.py`,
`session.py`, and `config.py` (comments only) all change, and `account/refresher.py`
and `tools/loop_lag.py` are new. **No config schema change**: the mark-tool fix is
internal to how a fill record is interpreted, and the account-refresh change adds
no config key — both mean an old config runs the new image and vice versa.

## Fixed

**`tools.mark` and `tools.desk` under-reported a filled day by half, because
the broker's open/close label is not a statement about Argus's intent.**
2026-08-25 filled two chained verticals — 7695/7700, then 7690/7695, whose
LONG leg is the first spread's SHORT leg. Chaining like this is the COMMON
case at `signal.width = 5.0` against SPX's 5-point ladder, not a tail. Argus
sent `BUY_TO_OPEN` on the second spread's long leg; the account was already
short that strike from the first spread, so tastytrade labelled the fill
**relative to the account's position** and echoed it back as `Buy to Close` —
on an order whose own identifier says `open`. `marker._decode_fill` compared
those two fields and dropped the record (seq 420587, a real 2-contract fill).
Downstream, `_net_legs` saw one leg symbol where a vertical needs two, the
spread's `opened_contracts` fell to 0, and a position that genuinely filled
scored as `SHAPE_UNFILLED` — re-priced counterfactually at settlement instead
of being counted. The day printed `1 idea(s) actually filled … 110.00` and a
desk total of `90.74` for a session that was `210.00` gross.

**The two fields were never in conflict** — one states what Argus was doing,
the other what the fill did to the account — and **the runtime was never
affected**: `store.signed_leg_delta` is deliberately open/close-agnostic, both
spreads were watched all session, the ledger/broker divergence check passed,
and no size rail was breached (the chained pair's $1,790 aggregate max loss
sits inside the welded `max_concurrent_positions × max_loss_per_position ≤
daily_max_loss`, 2 × 1250 = 2500). This was a scoring-layer defect only, in
the tool that is Phase 1's product.

**Fixed by asking about the CASH SIGN and the STRIKE instead of the suffix** —
neither is relabelled by the broker. `outcome._role_problems` resolves each
fill to the leg it moved (its OCC strike against the candidate key's two
strikes, via the new `occ.occ_strike`) and checks that the leg moved premium
the way that role requires: an opening short leg collects, an opening long leg
pays, a close reverses both. This is strictly stronger than the check it
replaced — it still refuses an exit's fills carrying an entry identifier (a
short leg *paying* premium on an open), and it additionally catches a fill on
a strike belonging to no leg of the candidate at all, which the old check
could not see and which a chained book makes a real question.

**The shape of the fix matters as much as the fix.** The first attempt kept
the check per-record in `_decode_fill` and returned `None` on a violation —
which the test suite caught: `test_a_leg_both_bought_and_sold_in_one_direction_
is_refused` went green for the wrong reason, because filtering the record at
decode meant the inconsistency was never seen downstream, and a
knowingly-broken execution scored CLEAN. **A rejection that deletes evidence
is the same defect in the other direction.** The check now lives in
`outcome.executions`, where a violation becomes an entry in
`Execution.problems` and the fill records survive.
→ [broker relabels leg actions](../decisions/2026-08-25-broker-relabels-leg-actions.md)

**A malformed execution could be indistinguishable from one that never
traded.** `_net_legs` reports zero contracts *alongside* its problems — it
cannot count complete spreads it could not assemble — so `Outcome.shape` fell
through `filled` onto `SHAPE_UNFILLED` for a position that genuinely had fills
but couldn't be assembled into a vertical. UNFILLED scores the idea
counterfactually at settlement on the proposed size, silently. **`shape` now
asks `problems` before `filled`**: a malformed execution reads
`SHAPE_UNPRICEABLE`, refuses to score (`pnl` is `None`, never a substitute
number), and prints under `mark`'s `!!` line instead of vanishing into the
counterfactual bucket. This is the general rail for every future cause of a
malformed execution, not just this one.
→ [same record](../decisions/2026-08-25-broker-relabels-leg-actions.md)

## Changed

**The account liveness refresh moved off the event loop — the other half of the
2026-08-10 lag ramp, the half nobody tested.** `AlertStreamerAccount._refresh_loop`
fires every 10s and did two HTTPS GETs **concurrently, on the event loop**. When
the connection pool is cold — its permanent state during a refresh-failure storm,
because a httpx timeout destroys the connection it fired on, not just the request —
each of those paid a full TCP+TLS handshake on the loop, at a CPU cost of the same
order as the 50ms heartbeat breach threshold. Measured 2026-08-25: 39.6ms p99 /
49.6ms max on-loop versus 1.11ms p99 threaded, a 35x reduction, on a fast Mac
against localhost — the droplet is slower and the gap is expected to be larger.
Breaches correlated with refresh failures at r = 0.695 on the droplet that day.

Those two requests now run **serialized, against one monotonic deadline, on a
dedicated single-worker `ThreadPoolExecutor`, through a synchronous `httpx2.Client`
that carries the existing session's bearer token** re-read fresh at every
dispatch. One OAuth registration, one login, one refresh-token path — the thread
holds no refresh token, no provider secret, and has no code path to
`/oauth/token`. Nothing anyio-shaped ever crosses the thread boundary (a `str`
does), so the 2026-07-21 hazard class — an anyio cancel scope unwound from the
wrong task — is **absent**, not managed. `staleness.account_refresh_timeout_s`
keeps its existing meaning (a total per-attempt budget) for both its callers, the
watchdog's book-confirm included; no gate, staleness bound, or fail-closed
contract changes. **No config key — the image deploys alone.**

**What this does NOT do, and no version of it could: save the connection pool.**
The pool still empties during a storm and every refresh is still cold. A cold
refresh simply stops costing event-loop time. The acceptance question is "did
`LoopHeartbeat.breach_count` fall while `account.refresh_failed` volume stayed
comparable?" — **not** "did the pool stay warm?", which will read as a failure of
a thing this change never claimed to fix.

Design-first, per CLAUDE.md's "this touches broker I/O on an always-on trading
process" bar: three shapes were evaluated and one approved before any code was
written. The build then found three things the design missed, the sharpest being
that `asyncio` **rebuilds** a `TimeoutError` crossing a thread/executor boundary
(`concurrent.futures.TimeoutError` *is* the builtin `TimeoutError` since 3.11, and
the converter reconstructs it as a bare `exc_class(*args)`) — so the normalized
timeout, the most common failure mode in a storm, would have arrived on the loop
stripped of its `leg`/`error_class` attributes and its `__cause__`, with no test
failing unless one asserted exactly that. Caught and fixed before commit, along
with two others (the in-flight guard must hold the `concurrent.futures.Future`,
not the asyncio wrapper that cancellation marks done while the thread runs on;
and `aclose()` cannot both queue the client close behind a wedged dispatch and
`cancel_futures=True`, which would cancel the close it just queued).

Full suite: **1,522 passing**, including 30 new cases in
`tests/test_account_refresher.py` and a `test_no_leaks.py` addition hammering the
refresher's exception path over two windows. Both AST guards untouched and green.
A local instrument (`tools/loop_lag.py`) reproduces the *direction* of the fix
against a self-signed cert over loopback but not the 35x magnitude (1.6x-5x on a
dev Mac) — expected on a one-cert loopback chain, and stated plainly so the local
number is never mistaken for confirmation.

**Status: DEPLOYED, not yet PROVEN.** The production reading — one full trading
session, `LoopHeartbeat.breach_count` against `account.refresh_failed` volume —
has not been taken. **Today (the day this shipped) is a confirmed `[events]`
blackout for the PCE release**, so `would_submit` is 0 all day and today cannot
supply that reading regardless of when the image goes out — the 2026-08-10
lag-ramp record's own warning, that a quiet day is not a fixed day, applies before

## Deploy

Rebuild only — no config to move with it.

```bash
cd /opt/argus
scripts/argus-deploy.sh --dry-run v0.3.10   # show the plan, touch nothing
scripts/argus-deploy.sh v0.3.10             # deploy it
```

Equivalent manually, per the runbook (§17): `git fetch --tags && git checkout
v0.3.10` (NO sudo on either), verify with `git describe --tags` and
`git status --short`, then `sudo docker compose build && sudo docker tag
argus:local argus:v0.3.10 && sudo systemctl restart argus.service`.

**Rollback needs only one half.** `argus.toml` is unchanged, so either
`git checkout v0.3.9` alone or `ARGUS_IMAGE_TAG=<v0.3.9 image>` alone is a
complete rollback. No dependency change; `requirements.txt` is untouched.

**The mark-tool fix touches the shadow-marking / desk-reporting tools, not the
trading path.** Nothing in `execution/`, `risk/`, or `broker/` changes for it,
and no rail's runtime behavior is affected — confirmed above, and by the fact
that no size, staleness, or halt test needed updating for it. `tests/` gained 9
cases for it, including an end-to-end replay of the actual 2026-08-25 day (both
real chained spreads, real fill prices) asserting the corrected total.

**The account-refresh change above does touch account state ingestion**
(`account/`, `session.py`) but changes no gate, no staleness bound, and no
fail-closed contract — only the implementation shape of one REST round trip.
`execution/`, `risk/`, and `broker/session.py`'s pool/keepalive configuration
are all untouched, and no existing staleness or halt test needed updating —
only extending, per the decision record's test-coverage section.

**2026-08-25 does not need re-marking.** Every run against it that day was
`--dry-run`; nothing was written to the ledger, so there is no stale record to
correct — running `tools.mark` for that date now against the new image scores
it properly for the first time.

## Scope

**Closes a reporting defect that made a filled day look like half of what it
was**, plus the general rail (`shape` consulting `problems` first) that
prevents the same silent miscount from any other cause. Both from one
2026-08-25 event.

**Also closes the other half of the 2026-08-10 lag ramp** — the account
liveness refresh's TLS handshakes, off the event loop. Design-approved
2026-08-26, built the same day. See **Changed**, above.

**One follow-on filed, not fixed here — and it is on the LIVE path, not the
reporting one.** `execution.models.infer_direction` reads the same relabelled
open/close suffix to decide an order's `direction`, and the same "any opening
leg wins" rule that happened to be correct for the entry side above is wrong
for its mirror: closing a spread whose long leg is another live spread's short
means selling to close a strike the account is net FLAT on, which can come
back `Sell to Open` — recording a closing order as `direction = 'open'`,
durably (`_upsert_order`'s `ON CONFLICT` never updates it). Harmless on a full
close; on a PARTIAL close it can turn a live half-spread into one reported
`malformed` instead of watched. **Not observed live** — no exit has yet fired
on a chained pair — and whether tastytrade relabels or outright REJECTS
`Sell to Close` on a flat position is unresolved and decides the shape of the
fix. Unlike the daily max-loss breaker, this one is provable in cert.
→ [relabelled closing legs](../decisions/2026-08-25-relabelled-closing-legs-open.md)

**The daily max-loss circuit breaker remains structurally inert in sandbox**,
unchanged by this release. Proving that rail out is still a live-flip gate.
