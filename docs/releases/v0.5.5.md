## Name

**Alnilam** (proposed), ε Orionis, Arabic *al-niẓām*, **"the string of pearls"** / "the
arrangement", the name of the asterism of the three belt stars, applied later to the middle
one. IAU-approved in 2016.
- **Sources, independent:** [Wikipedia, List of Arabic star
  names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Arabic_star_names) and [Wikipedia,
  Alnilam](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Alnilam) (the *al-niẓām* / *naẓm* gloss); [Wiktionary,
  Alnilam](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/Alnilam) and the [Jena star-name
  wiki](https://xing.fmi.uni-jena.de/mediawiki/index.php/Alnilam). Found in one web search
  each; the four are not fully independent of one another (the IAU WGSN bulletin is their
  common root), so treat the second-source check as **light** and re-check before spending.
- **How it qualifies, and the weakness:** **not literally an eye.** It qualifies loosely, by
  tradition, on Vindemiatrix's and Mirzam's standing (a named part of a watcher's sky), and
  that is thin. Say so when choosing. **Found outside the pool, which is empty; it spends no
  reservation.**
- **The fit is the release's shape.** The selector recorded the one pair it chose and its
  near miss; every other strike's quote was dropped. The ladder row is a string of pearls:
  each strike on each side, in order, with its bid, ask and mid-implied delta, laid out
  once a minute. The second half (a calibration that is re-read rather than trusted) has no
  particular claim on the name.

**Alternative: Canopus** (α Carinae), the navigators' star, used by spacecraft star trackers
to fix and re-fix their heading. It fits the recalibration half, not the ladder half. Its
etymology is disputed (often given as the helmsman of Menelaus's fleet), so it **needs two
sources before it is spent**; not checked here.

**Richard's choice.**

## Rules to carry

- **A telemetry row never costs a decision.** `maybe_record` swallows every exception and
  claims its minute first; it is called before the strategy and its result is discarded.
- **`signal.ladder` is written, never read, by `src/`.** Only the orchestrator imports the
  module, and the category string lives in the ladder module alone (AST-pinned).
- **z99 and alpha are calibration outputs, never hand-tuned**; a rerun ships in either
  direction (the amendment), pending Richard's confirmation of the rule.
- **The calibration sample is non-event days.** FOMC, discretionary and CPI/NFP/PCE release
  days stay out whether or not Argus trades them; putting them back is a decision.
- **An empty or failed FRED answer is never "no release days".**
- **Rerun the tool before any change to z99 or alpha**; lowering k still needs a rerun and
  a decision record.

## Why it's safe

- **The one trading change moves the boundary toward flattening**, the conservative
  direction: less is left to expiry. The touch stop stays live to the close either way, and
  the worst case of a deferral is unchanged and inside `daily_max_loss`.
- **The cost is small and stated** (about −$0.3 to −$0.8/day); the model is at or above the
  data at the new boundary.
- **The ladder cannot reach a decision.** It runs before the strategy but returns nothing,
  and every failure is its own. Four mutations (no minute gate, no exception guard, the
  feed delta instead of the mid, a raw bid) are caught by `tests/test_ladder_telemetry.py`.
  The hostile-quote tests now also assert the minute's ladder row survives.
- **The write load is small:** about 2.5 MB a session against about 20 MB for the live DB
  today, under 1% of the write rate.
- **The tool change is not in the process.**
- **Evidence limits, stated:** 30 sessions of one regime, a fit that one session moved, and
  model marks for the flatten's price. No `flatten_deferral_outcome` row exists yet.

## What this release does NOT do

- **It does not change k, the strike-touch stop, the caps, the side rule, the profit target
  (still off), the loss budget, or any size rail.** It adds no config key and no migration.
- **It does not read the ladder.** No reader tool, no change to selection, pricing or sizing.
- **It does not fix `release_days.py`.** `docs/research/2026-09-28-audit-due-diligence-queries/release_days.py`
  classifies a release day as a blacked-out day that is not FOMC or discretionary. From
  2026-09-30 it will count traded release days as "other". It needs the same FRED source
  before its next rerun is read. Not fixed here.
- **It does not settle the two open sign-offs** (the calibration exclusion and the
  adoption rule).
- **It does not make the deferral evidence-complete**; the first live outcomes are still owed.
- **It does not change the release-day rule or its −$600 abort**, and it does not lift the
  release-day calibration caveat: the deferral runs on release days with the non-event fit.
