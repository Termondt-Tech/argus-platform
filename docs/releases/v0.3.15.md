# v0.3.15 — Alnasl

*TAGGED, RELEASED, AND DEPLOYED 2026-09-03 ~07:58 EDT — before the 10:00 ET
entry window, no session in progress. Prepared 2026-09-02 from what is on
`main`. Six pieces of work,
all raised by or found alongside the 2026-09-01 pass over v0.3.13's second
live-fire day, and independently reconfirmed live on 2026-09-02 against the
still-deployed v0.3.14 image (below). Four fixes correct a number or a verdict
the exit path was trusting wrongly: a walk that repriced concluded from one rung
about a spread that several rungs share, so an exit that had already succeeded
was recorded as a failure; the credit stop's comparison fired on floating-point
noise, eleven orders of magnitude below the smallest price difference that can
exist; the credit stop's basis was the filled order's LIMIT rather than what the
fills actually collected; and the flatten's one-shot override was spent by a
look rather than a send, burning the position's last resort on an order that was
never sent. The other two close a `/status` observability gap — a build version
finally answerable without git archaeology, and every held-but-unmanaged
position and abandoned order now visible on the page instead of reading calm.*

*Named per the Argus's Eyes convention (`docs/releases/README.md`). **Alnasl** —
γ² Sagittarii, from the asterism *Al-Kuha* ("The Eye"), aligned along the
Archer's own sightline — was reserved in the pool for **"order routing /
execution precision: a release focused on fill-price accuracy, slippage, or
entry/exit timing rather than a gap in what is being watched."** The four exit-
path fixes are that reading almost to the word — what a walk concludes from a
fill, what counts as a breach, what the fill price actually was, and when an
override may be spent. The `/status` work sits alongside rather than against the
name: it watches nothing new, it only stops the page from staying calm about
degradation the watchdog and the residual registry already know about.
**Aldebaran and Rastaban stay reserved** — neither a clean, steady re-watch nor a
second independent verification loop is what any of this is.*

**`src/` only. No new config key, and MIGRATION 0005 is additive.**
`execution/loop.py`, `execution/watchdog.py`, `execution/store.py`,
`execution/build.py`, `execution/zerobid.py`, `execution/residual_orders.py`,
`session.py`, `status.py`, `app.py`, `tools/desk.py`, and a new `_version.py`
change. `db/migrations/0005_order_leg_fill_prices.sql` adds two columns to
`order_legs` (`priced_quantity`, `priced_notional`) and runs from the image at
startup — no backfill, no second writer, and a row written before this release
reads back bit-for-bit as it always has (both columns zero, which falls back to
the limit basis). `argus.toml`/`argus.dev.toml` are untouched. **Image rebuild
required; config does not move — the first uncoupled deploy since v0.3.10**,
after four consecutive releases that each forced a config move. The build step
itself changes (see Deploy): a build ARG now stamps the image with its own
version, and the runbook's build command is no longer optional to get right.

## Fixed

**A walk that repriced concluded about one rung, and an exit that had already
succeeded was recorded as a failure.** On 2026-09-01, `put-7615-7610`'s exit
triggered, opened order 1430426, repriced once to 1430433, and the broker
refused the replacement with `reject_reason: 'Received a fill on the original
order'` — the original had filled, 2 contracts at a 1.50 debit, spread flat. The
walk recorded `REJECTED` → `ExitOutcome.NOT_RESOLVED`, spent the position's one
exit attempt, paged the operator, and wrote `filled_contracts: 0` for a spread
that filled 2. `_reprice` moves `state.broker_order_id`/`state.last` onto the
replacement as soon as the replace is *acknowledged* and never inspects
`replaced.status`; the next `_observe` read the replacement, found it terminal,
and `_resolve` took the verdict **rung-scoped**. CLAUDE.md already states the
rule this broke — *"the FILLED check stays identifier-WIDE, a fill on any rung
means the spread is flat"* — and the codebase already implemented it in two
places (`_await_settled`, `_recover`) and needed it in three. **Hit 1 of 3
successful replaces that day (33%), correlated by construction**: a reprice goes
out exactly when a fill is imminent. **Fixed**: `_resolve`'s terminal-non-fill
branch now asks `by_identifier` before it concludes, covering `REJECTED` and
`CANCELLED_ELSEWHERE` alike (the identical shape — a terminal non-fill on one
rung while another rung filled), ungated on `state.steps` (the broker does not
enforce identifier uniqueness), and a probe that cannot itself be answered FALLS
BACK to the verdict already reached rather than inventing a new one. The entry
mirror shares `_reprice` and is covered by the same fix — never a
position-safety hole (`positions` books from the fill feed, independently of
the walk), but it mis-scored `desk`/`mark` and would have fed the open
consecutive-rejection back-off decision a false count of "the broker refused."
Shipped with the fix for the page that would have explained the incident:
`_record_exit` dropped `reject_reason`, now held structurally by an AST sweep
across every per-position alert that names an outcome — which caught a third
silent page, `_flatten_may_act`, on its first run. **What was NOT changed**: the
one-attempt rule, `_await_settled`'s FILLED-wide/settle-narrow asymmetry, and
`filled_contracts`' across-rungs under-count. Not observed to fire again on
2026-09-02: two replaces that day, both succeeded, no rejection in the trail.
→ [a terminal rung is not a verdict](../decisions/2026-09-02-a-terminal-rung-is-not-a-verdict.md)

**A credit-multiple stop fired on 4.4e-16 of floating-point noise and closed a
position that expired worthless.** `call-7670-7675` was stopped out at
`cost_to_close: 1.6000000000000005` against `credit_stop: 1.6` — eleven orders
of magnitude below the 0.05 tick, and below any price difference that can
exist. Realized −160.00 on a spread that settled out of the money. **`Decimal`
was not the fix**: `float(Decimal("0.8")) * 2.0` is exactly the double nearest
`1.6`; the noise is upstream, in `spread_mid`'s four-quote arithmetic, which the
ENTRY path shares and which prices the exit order — a quantity with three
consumers, one of which is a threshold test. **Fixed**:
`TriggerThresholds.credit_breached`, a sibling to `underlying_breached`,
requires an OBSERVABLE margin — `cost_to_close - credit_stop >= PRICE_EPSILON`,
a new public constant in `zerobid.py` derived from that module's own
`_INPUT_PRECISION` so the two cannot drift, sized off the price grid rather than
off `sys.float_info`. **`underlying_breached` deliberately does not take it** —
no equality-by-construction on that comparison, and a tolerance there would
only delay the underlying backstop — held by a test, not a comment. The stop
now fires *strictly less often*, monotonically. **Confirmed reproducing on the
still-deployed v0.3.14 image the very next day** (2026-09-02): 2 of 2
`credit_multiple` stops that day fired on 4.441e-16 and 6.661e-16 respectively —
worse than the 1-of-5 rate the fix was written against, and verified directly
against the day's own numbers that `PRICE_EPSILON = 1e-6` would have stopped
neither.
→ [the credit stop's two wrong numbers](../decisions/2026-09-02-the-credit-stop-basis-and-its-comparison.md)

**The credit stop was measured against the filled order's LIMIT, not what the
fills actually collected — known since 2026-08-20, and now a realized dollar
consequence rather than a projected one.** A limit order fills at the limit or
better, so `OpenSpread.entry_credit` (read from `orders.limit_price`) was always
LOW by construction, tightening `credit_stop = credit_multiple × entry_credit`
by the same margin. `put-7630-7625` on 2026-09-01 recorded 0.45 and collected
0.50 on both contracts; it stopped at 0.925 against a stop of 0.90, where the
true basis puts the stop at 1.00 and it is never stopped out at all. Deferred
for two months as "the safe direction and not worth a migration" —
2026-09-01 put a realized close on the other side of that judgment. **Fixed**:
migration 0005 adds `priced_quantity`/`priced_notional` to `order_legs`, written
by `store._apply_leg` in the SAME transaction as `applied_quantity` and
`positions` from the per-fill price `OrderLeg.fill_price` already computes for
the slice being booked. `entry_credit` is now the CONTRACTS-WEIGHTED realized
price, aggregated per leg across every OPENING order sharing a trade idea's
identifier — not the newest order alone, because a walk that reprices fills
across several `broker_order_id`s under one identifier (proven 2026-09-01:
1429812 filled 1 contract, 1429813 filled 1) and a per-order price still misses
the earlier rung. **Zero on both columns — every row written before this
release — reads back as the limit basis, bit for bit**: no backfill needed, since
Argus trades 0DTE only and every prior position is already settled within its
own session. **A non-positive realized basis keeps the position WATCHED on the
limit and pages** — routing it to `malformed` would strip a live vertical's
triggers, its flatten, its `/status` row, and its concurrency slot over a price
anomaly. **The `credit_multiple` prohibition NARROWS rather than lifts**: a
conclusion may now be drawn from positions where `entry_credit_basis =
realized`; a `limit`-basis position is EXCLUDED, not corrected. **Confirmed
reproducing on the still-deployed v0.3.14 image the very next day**
(2026-09-02): 2 of 4 filled positions again understated by exactly one tick
(0.05), one of them running a stop 7% tighter than its own fills justified —
across both days the count now stands at 5 of 12 filled positions, all
understating in the same direction by the same amount.
→ [the credit stop's two wrong numbers](../decisions/2026-09-02-the-credit-stop-basis-and-its-comparison.md)

**The flatten's one-shot past-the-mark override was spent by a LOOK, not a
SEND — burning the position's last resort on an order that was never sent.**
`FLATTEN_BEFORE_CLOSE` grants a position whose exit attempts are already spent
one further send past the mark, the single override on a stated
non-negotiable. The spend was claimed the moment the legs had QUOTES
(`watchdog.py`), but `build_order` can still fail after that — `_limit_price`
refuses a net mid that rounds to 0.00 at the tick, which is the flatten's MODAL
regime, since a winning 0DTE spread is nearly worthless by 15:30. Observed on 2
of 2 flattens on 2026-09-01; harmless only because neither position had spent
its ordinary attempt first, a property of that day and not of the mechanism.
**Fixed**: the override is now claimed only at the SEND — inside the same
one-way gate `DuplicateOrderGuard` already licenses, so a build failure between
the quote check and the send restores the position's full allowance rather than
burning it on nothing. A `NOT_ATTEMPTED` result is now structurally incapable of
describing a sent order (an AST test pins it), and the paging is once per
position across both refusal shapes rather than once per unbuildable tick.
Shipped in the same change, on a second sign-off: the operator page for this
refusal used to read `ValueError: net mid 0.0 rounds to 0.00 ...` — accurate,
and indistinguishable from a genuine builder defect except by matching prose,
which this codebase already refuses to key behaviour on (`Order.reject_reason`'s
own rule). The refusal now names itself as a designed refusal, never a crash,
on both the entry and exit paths. **Confirmed reproducing on the
still-deployed v0.3.14 image the very next day** (2026-09-02): both of that
day's flattens raised the identical `ValueError` — 4 of 4 across two days,
settling the question the design record posed as a prediction: this is the
flatten's modal regime, not its tail. Both closed correctly on retry; nothing
about the confirmation argues for pricing an unpriceable spread.
→ [the flatten's override is spent by a SEND, not by a look](../decisions/2026-09-02-the-flatten-allowance-is-spent-by-a-send.md)

**`/status` can finally say what build is actually running, without git
archaeology.** During the chunk 1-4 cutover, `main` did not contain the chunks
and nothing at runtime said so — caught only by the luck of a pre-flight check.
**Fixed**: a build ARG (`ARGUS_VERSION`, computed by `git describe --tags
--always --dirty` on the HOST, before the daemon is invoked) stamps a new
`argus/_version.py` at image build time; the runtime image still carries no
`.git` and no git binary. `/status` (JSON and HTML) and the startup log now show
it next to environment/uptime. An unstamped build reports `unknown` — honest,
never a crash, never a blank field. A `-dirty` suffix (tracked files modified at
build time — routine on the droplet, where `argus.toml` is both tracked and
edited in place for `[server] bind_host`) renders amber rather than red: *the
running code is not exactly what that tag says*, not *something is wrong*. The
runbook's build step is now `docker compose build --build-arg
ARGUS_VERSION=$(git describe --tags --always --dirty)`, not the plain `docker
compose build` documented before, and states explicitly that `argus.service`
has no build coupling — a bare `systemctl restart` reattaches to whichever
image was last built, silently, with no error, which is exactly the
stale-but-not-crashing gap this field exists to close. Build first, tag, then
restart, then read the version back — the runbook now gives the two-line
confirmation (`journalctl` + a `curl` against `/status`) to do that.
→ Task 2, [`docs/tasks/argus_status_version_and_blackout_expiry_implementation_prompt.md`](../tasks/argus_status_version_and_blackout_expiry_implementation_prompt.md)

**`/status` now warns before the macro-event blackout list runs dry, instead of
an exhausted list reading as a genuinely quiet day.** `blackout_dates.txt` /
`fomc_dates.txt` end 2026-12-23 with no 2027 dates seeded yet (source has not
published them), and nothing inside the process could previously tell the
difference between "no blackout today" and "no blackout data left." **Fixed**:
`/status` computes the blackout list's max date and warns — JSON field and an
amber/red HTML badge, matching the existing staleness/heartbeat convention —
once that date is within `BLACKOUT_WARN_DAYS` (30, chosen against the repo's
existing staleness-threshold order of magnitude). Silent in the common case;
this does not seed 2027 dates or touch blackout logic itself, only visibility
that the list needs seeding before it runs out.
→ Task 1, [`docs/tasks/argus_status_version_and_blackout_expiry_implementation_prompt.md`](../tasks/argus_status_version_and_blackout_expiry_implementation_prompt.md)

**`/status` now names every held-but-unmanaged position and abandoned order —
closing two open decisions the page has carried since 2026-08-11 and
2026-08-30.** A `positions` row no order explains, an order whose legs cannot be
reassembled into a spread, and a residual order P5's recheck is watching or
would-cancel all paged Tier 1 once per episode and then read as an ordinary
open position or vanished from the page entirely — the worst state the system
can put a position in, invisible on the one surface an operator checks from a
phone. **Fixed**: one new `unmanaged` section, two rows — **Unwatched
positions** (`unattributed` / `malformed`, red, with one amber exception for the
ordinary ~0.25s mid-fill settle window a multi-leg order's two legs book across)
and **Residual orders** (amber, escalating red only on a read gap the broker
will neither confirm dead nor licence a cancel for — a positive `would_cancel`
vs `cancel` mode shown on every row, so an operator can tell whether P5 is
watching or intervening). Both are LIVE reads off the watchdog and the residual
registry — never an audit row — following the `impaired_positions()` /
`spent_exit_attempts()` precedent, and neither read can take the scan or the
page down on failure. Named `unmanaged` rather than `degraded_positions`
specifically because `_impaired_cell` already uses "degraded" for something
strictly milder. **Designed first and signed off before any code**, all six
open design questions resolved with no amendments. Shipped alongside a smaller,
independent finding raised while shipping the credit-stop fixes: the session
seam feeding the existing spent-exit row silently dropped three of
`SpentExitAttempt`'s eight fields, so `/status` rendered `mark: unpriced` for
every spent exit, always — fixed as its own commit, ahead of and as the working
precedent for this section's two new seams, with the sibling `evaluability` seam
confirmed already complete.
→ [`/status` names what Argus is holding but not managing](../decisions/2026-09-02-status-names-what-is-unmanaged.md),

## Deploy

Rebuild only — no config change, no migration to stage ahead of the restart
(0005 runs from the image at startup). **The build command itself changes and
is not optional to get right:**

```bash
cd /opt/argus
git checkout v0.3.15
git status --short                          # must be clean (or the expected argus.toml edit)

sudo docker compose build --build-arg ARGUS_VERSION=$(git describe --tags --always --dirty)
sudo docker tag argus:local argus:v0.3.15
sudo docker images argus                    # confirm the version tag is there

sudo systemctl restart argus.service

# Confirm what is actually running — either read alone is enough.
sudo journalctl -u argus.service -n 50 | grep 'argus build'
curl -fsS -H "Authorization: Bearer $ARGUS_KILL_TOKEN" \
     http://<tailscale-ip>:8443/status | grep -o '"version":"[^"]*"'
```

**Omitting `--build-arg ARGUS_VERSION=...` builds fine and reports `"unknown"`**
on `/status` and at startup — never a failure, never a blank field, but not the
answer this feature exists to give. **A bare `systemctl restart argus.service`
with no fresh build silently reattaches to whichever image was last built** —
`argus.service` has no `build` step and no `--build` flag and needs no change
for any of this. Build, tag, restart, then read the version back, in that
order — the runbook now states this explicitly (§17, Build and Deploy). Do not
move the build arg into `docker-compose.yml`'s `args:` keyed off `.env`; that
reads a file the deploy does not touch and the first release where the code
moves and `.env` does not produces an image confidently labelled with the
PREVIOUS version.

**Rollback is `git checkout v0.3.14`, image-only** — no config to revert, no
migration to reverse. Migration 0005 is additive; a prior image simply never
reads or writes `priced_quantity`/`priced_notional`, and every existing
`order_legs` row already has both at their default of `0`.

**After deploy, what to look for:**

- `/status`'s "Build" row (and the startup log's `argus build:` line) showing
  `v0.3.15` (or `v0.3.15-dirty` if `argus.toml`'s droplet-local edit is present,
  which is expected and renders amber, not red).
- No `credit_multiple` trigger firing with `cost_to_close` and `credit_stop`
  differing by less than roughly $2.50 a contract (`PRICE_EPSILON`'s bound, in
  points). A genuine one-tick-or-larger breach must still fire — confirm at
  least one ordinary stop-out still triggers this deploy day.
- A `FLATTEN_BEFORE_CLOSE` refusal on an unbuildable mid, if one occurs, now
  reading as a named refusal rather than a bare `ValueError`, and the same
  position's override still available on the next priceable tick.
- Any exit-side rejection alert now carrying a `reject_reason` string rather
  than the fixed `"broker rejected the order"` fallback, on the next exit
  rejection of any kind (rare — only two have ever been observed).
- The `/status` positions block showing the new **Unwatched positions** /
  **Residual orders** rows, both reading `None` (not blank, not `()`) outside a
  session and `()` once one starts with nothing degraded.
- New `order.fill`/`walk.finished` records carrying through to a durable
  `entry_credit` visibly different from `orders.limit_price` on any position
  that fills with price improvement.
- The `/status` blackout-runway field staying silent (healthy runway) — it is
  not expected to warn until within 30 days of 2026-12-23.

**Deliberately NOT in this release**, reported and left standing:

- **The owed Fix D settle allowance for `watchdog.unevaluable`**, now sized at
  ~20.0s from a second day of measurement (a 0DTE position with no market
  trigger evaluated for up to 21.9s on 2026-09-02, both market triggers dark at
  once). A new time-based `[watchdog]` key; must not reuse `book_settle_s`.
- **A settle allowance for `watchdog.leg_imbalance`**, the last book-shape page
  with no allowance and no evidence test — paged falsely on 2026-09-02 against
  a spread's own closing fill, the same shape `unwatchable`/`held_but_unbooked`
  already handle.
- **A submit-time refusal must not report as an absence.** `order.submit_failed`
  carries the broker's exact reason (the failing symbol, on 2026-09-02) and it
  never reaches `/status` or the alert — `instrument_validation_failed` and a
  genuine `timeout_error` are indistinguishable to an operator, both arriving as
  `no_order_exists` with `reject_reason: null` after paying a full reconnect and
  back-off built for a transient. Cert-only in its current cause; the reporting
  gap is environment-independent.
- **`execution.walk_source` / `watchdog.trigger_source`** still carry
  SANDBOX-ENTRY values, and the owed data-backed pass now also owes a
  re-derivation of `exit_walk_min_steps` / `exit_walk_away_pct` from real
  `walk.finished` outcome mix.
- **The walk's floor has no width cap** where `_cross_limit` does. Unreachable
  at `exit_walk_min_steps = 1` (v0.3.14) but real if that key is ever raised.
- **`infer_direction` and the relabelled closing legs.** 2026-09-01 proved the
  never-fired exit-on-a-chained-pair condition fires (three times) and that
  tastytrade relabels rather than rejects. Whether to change where direction
  comes from is still an open decision.
- **The open decision on backing off by COUNT of consecutive terminal
  rejections.** This release's first fix removes one false-positive source from
  that eventual count, but the decision itself is not made here.
- **`working_orders_unread`'s known blind spot**, unchanged: one failed
  `get_live_orders()` poll still blinds BOTH P4's working-order count and the
  new residual row at once — the row keeps its last-known state and cannot show
  that the answer has gone stale.
- **Whether `credit_multiple = 2.0` is the right threshold for 0DTE.** Raised by
  the 2026-09-02 pass; this release makes the question answerable on a correct
  basis, it does not answer it.
- Any move toward a confirmation-gated exit RE-ATTEMPT. Explicitly not this
  release, on any fix.

**The daily max-loss circuit breaker remains structurally inert in sandbox**,
unchanged by this release. Proving that rail out is still a live-flip gate.
