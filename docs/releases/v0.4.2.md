## Name

**`Gorgonea Tertia`** — ρ Persei, the third of the four stars of the **Gorgon's
head** in Perseus, the asterism whose brightest member is **Algol**, "the Demon's
Eye," spent on v0.3.1.

Found outside the pool; Rastaban remains the only reservation and its
dual-watchdog / consensus reading does not fit — nothing here adds a second
verification loop. Qualifies by **asterism**, the standing already given to
**Grumium** (one of the four stars of Draco's head, where Eltanin and Rastaban are
the eyes) and **Alhena** (the same Arabic asterism grouping as Tejat). It also
deliberately claims a companion of an already-spent name, the pattern used twice
before on purpose — **Alcor → Mizar** (v0.3.5 → v0.3.19) and **Zubeneschamali →
Zubenelgenubi** (v0.3.20 → v0.3.21).

**The fit is Perseus's shield, not Medusa's gaze.** Perseus could not look at the
Gorgon directly and survive, so he fought her by watching her reflection in a
polished shield — **he acted on an instrument rather than on the subject.** That
is the release's one real finding, exactly: `tools.desk` is the mirror the EOD SOP
reads the trading day through, and on 2026-09-14 it stopped tracking. A position's
credit stop fired, four exit attempts failed at the transport layer, the 15:30
flatten died the same way — and the mirror showed a quiet held row, marked at
11:34:10, four and a half hours stale, with no exit named anywhere on the page.
Everything Argus did was correct and none of it reached the surface it is read
through. **A reflection you fight by is only as good as its refresh rate**, and ρ
Persei is itself a **semiregular variable** — it changes while you watch it, which
is precisely what the frozen reader failed to notice its subject doing.

**Distinct from every spent eye**, which is why it earns the name rather than
doubling one: **Algol** (same asterism) is the eye that visibly *blinks* — it went
to a backstop that went fully dark; **Gomeisa** (v0.4.1) is the eye that is open
and running and simply *pointed at the wrong subject*; **Alcor** is the *test* of
eyesight; **Mizar** and **Albireo** are one point *resolving into two*. This one is
none of those: the instrument was pointed correctly, was not dark, and was
reporting a real number — it had just stopped being *updated*, because the subject
moved to a place the instrument's query did not reach.

## Scope

### 1. `tools.desk` hid a spent exit — FIXED (the release's only `src/` change)

→ [the desk view hides a spent exit](../decisions/2026-09-14-the-desk-view-hides-a-spent-exit.md)

Found in the EOD SOP review of v0.4.1's 2026-09-14 cert session. On that day
`20260914-call-7625-7630`'s credit stop fired at 11:33:10 against a 1.60 mark on a
1.40 stop; every send failed at the transport layer (`walk.disconnected` × 7,
`order.submit_failed` × 4 — cert's order API was unreachable for the position's
entire exit lifecycle); the attempt was spent at 11:34:56; the 15:30 flatten fired
to the second, claimed its one-shot override, and died the same way. **The report
showed a quiet held row marked `@11:34:10` and "nothing was closed before
expiry".**

**Two defects, one page.**

- **`read_marks` read one mark category.** `watchdog.check` *stops* the instant an
  exit is spent and `watchdog.check_spent` takes over (`_attempted` is never
  pruned, so the move is one-way). Reading the first alone froze the mark at the
  last tick *before* the exit died and discarded **10,656** later marks — the one
  row a desk most needs to watch was the one row that stopped updating, at the
  exact instant it started to matter.
- **The exit outcome was read on the wrong branch.** `read_exits` worked and
  `DeskDay.exits` was correct; it was consulted only on the **closed** row, where
  the outcome is `filled` and adds nothing you cannot read off the fill two columns
  over — and never on the held row, where it is the whole story.

**Nobody wrote a bug.** Both halves were correct when written; `watchdog.check_spent`
arrived 2026-08-28 from the pass whose entire point was that a held, triggered
spread had gone 76 minutes with nothing able to say what it was worth. That pass
gave the mark to the Slack pages. `/status` was the second surface to miss it
(fixed 2026-09-02); the desk view was the third.

**Two rules, and the `None`-defaults rule from the 2026-09-02 `/status` record
does not reach here** — there is no copy and no default:

- **A reader that names ONE audit category makes a closed-world claim about where
  a fact lives, and the runtime is free to add another.** A category filter cannot
  fail loudly: the rows are simply not selected, the query is valid, and the number
  produced is a real number from a real record — just the wrong one. Hence
  `MARK_CATEGORIES`, the claim stated in one place instead of implied at a call
  site.
- **A fact is most needed on the branch where it is least expected.** This is why
  the failure was *reassuring* rather than merely absent: the page did not say
  "unknown," it said nothing was closed, in the tone of an ordinary hold.

**Built:** both mark categories in one `ORDER BY seq` query, newest look winning
whichever it came from; `Mark.exit_outcome` read **before** the
`cost_to_close is None` guard, because whether a position has an attempt left is
not a question about quotes — the asymmetric case (the last *priced* look predates
the spend) is the one a naive version gets wrong by answering `None`, i.e.
"ordinary hold"; `read_exits` returns **every** attempt as a frozen `ExitAttempt`
carrying the exit's own `reason` **and** the broker's verbatim `reject_reason`,
two fields never one, on the `mutation_error` precedent; and the spent state sits
on the held **row** (`EXIT SPENT (nothing_at_broker)`), not only in a section, with
a new `EXITS THAT DID NOT CLOSE THE POSITION` table and a `WHY` list below it.

**The row's note is deliberately precise about what SPENT does and does not mean.**
A draft read "nothing will submit for it again," which is **false** for
`NOTHING_AT_BROKER` and `CROSS_UNPRICEABLE` (both in `_RETRYABLE_EXITS`) and for
the flatten's one-shot allowance. It now names the budget and the allowance and
says MANUAL only where neither applies — a monitoring surface that overstates a
rail is the same defect one direction over.

**The 2026-09-14 day itself is the strongest exit-path evidence this project has**,
and is the substantive argument for flipping now: the credit stop fired on the
right number past its debounce, `GuardUnreachable` was absorbed **without halting
the process** (the exact failure that killed 2026-09-03), the one-attempt rule
held, `check_spent` kept evaluating, the `_SPENT` escalation bounded the silence,
the flatten claimed its override, and P5 adopted an abandoned order end to end.
The instrument the EOD SOP reads it through showed none of it.

**The P&L is not evidence about anything.** Closing at 11:33 realizes −$180; SPX
settled at 7620.995 and the 7625 short call expired 4 points OTM, so the position
paid **+$140** and the day was **+$132.91 net of fees, not the −$187.09 the page
totalled**. The failure made money for the second time in two weeks. One
narrow-range session is where a stop is maximally wrong.

### 2. The last two go-live decisions, written down — G1 accepted, C5 accepted and BROADENED

→ [`docs/go-live-findings.md`](../go-live-findings.md)

**G1 — accepted in writing (Richard, 2026-09-14).** The daily max-loss breaker goes
live unproven; cert cannot add to its coverage and never will. The substantive part
is a distinction that had not been drawn anywhere: **the rail has four parts and
they go live on different days.** The anchor and the measurement are exercised on
day 1 within seconds of the first account read; same-day rehydration only if the
process restarts; **the trip is not day 1 and probably not for weeks.** That
follows from the welded invariant itself — `max_concurrent_positions = 1` and
`daily_max_loss = 950.00 = 1 × max_loss_per_position`, so with one position a day
the loss only reaches 950 if that single spread goes to near its full width. The
realistic day-1 outcome is *"measured correctly, did not trip"*, which is a real
result for two of four parts and silence on the third. **Do not read a quiet first
week as the rail being proven.**

The acceptance also promotes the highest-value day-1 check: **`daily_pnl` must not
be identically zero.** It is `0.0` in cert by construction, and if live `/status`
shows `0.0` with a position open and marked away from entry, the rail is inert in
live too — that is the gate firing, not a quiet day. And it records the three-number
cross-check, including the one that **will not match and must not be expected to**:
`tools.desk` is per-position from fills and watchdog marks, `DailyPnl` is
whole-account NLV delta at broker marks with fees on the broker's schedule. Without
that written down, day 1 manufactures a false alarm.

**C5 — accepted, and broadened.** The checklist item was narrower than the risk it
was standing for. The class is **"the broker will not take a closing order while a
position needs closing,"** and it has two members with identical harm: a stuck
`Cancel Requested` (still never seen) and **submissions failing outright**, which
was observed for 4h25m on 2026-09-14. Accepted on three grounds — bounded by the
spread width and therefore by `max_loss_per_position`; the machine's refusal to
retry is a deliberate rail, not a defect to soften; and the residual exposure is
covered by a human.

The monitoring plan is one signal — `watchdog.exit_spent_still_held`, arriving as
roughly one Slack alert every 15 minutes through B1's per-position throttle — and a
four-step manual-close SOP whose second step is the one that matters: **check
whether anything is still working at the broker before sending anything.** A human
closing on top of an order that can still fill re-opens the position inverted on
expiry day, which is the exact harm the machine's one-attempt rule exists to
prevent and which the operator inherits the moment they take over. Two outcomes
that page but are **not** this risk are carved out: `NOT_ATTEMPTED` (the flatten's
modal regime) and the two `_RETRYABLE_EXITS` members.

**Consequence for the header:** provenance updated to **21 closed, 5 partly
closed, 3 open — none blocking**, and the status box now reads *nothing left is a
build, nothing left is a read, and nothing left is a decision.*

### 3. The cutover runbook stated an NLV floor that does not exist — CORRECTED

→ [`docs/live-cutover-runbook.md`](../live-cutover-runbook.md) §1.2 (new)

§1's divergence table read *"implies an NLV floor of 950 / 0.03 = $31,667 — below
it nothing trades."* That divides `max_loss_per_position` by `position_size_pct`,
and **those two rails are applied to different quantities**: `PreTradeGate` bounds
the *proposal's* defined risk (`contracts × (width − credit) × 100`) by
`position_size_pct × NLV`, while `size_contracts` floors at one lot, so the gate is
what refuses. The real floor is **graded and depends on the candidate** — ~$15,000
for one 5-wide lot, ~$30,000 for two, and $31,667 only for the *widest* candidate
`max_width` admits.

**Consequence if it had stood, in both directions:** a live account between $15k
and $31k would have read as "below the floor, do not flip" when Argus would in fact
have traded it correctly at reduced size — and, the other way, `size_cap_exceeded`
on wide candidates would have read as a day-1 defect rather than as the rail
working. P7 is downgraded from a hard precondition to a sizing expectation.

## Deploy

**`build: YES`, `config: NO`, `restart: YES`.**

**This is not a docs-only release and must not be deployed as one.**
`src/argus/tools/desk.py` lives inside the image — the EOD SOP reaches it through
`docker compose run --rm argus python -m argus.tools.desk` — so **the fix does not
exist on the droplet until the image is rebuilt.** A `config:`-class deploy would
leave the old desk view running against the new documentation describing it.

```bash
cd /opt/argus && scripts/argus-deploy.sh v0.4.2
```

**The restart is behaviourally inert for trading and is still a restart.** No
trading-path code changed — no `src/` file outside `tools/` is touched, no config
key moves, no migration — but a build restarts the process, which re-reads the
durable kill switch from the audit log, re-reconciles against the broker at process
start, and rehydrates the day's breaker state. Deploy it **outside the session
window**, as usual.

**Sequence it BEFORE the cutover, not after.** The entire reason item 1 was worth
fixing is that `tools.desk` is the instrument the first live day will be read
through. Deploying it after the flip means the first live EOD read — the one with a
real fill, real fees and a real NLV — comes back through the version that hides a
spent exit.

**Post-deploy assertions:**

- `/status` unchanged in shape — no new fields, no new rows, no config fingerprint
  change (config is untouched, so the sha must be identical to v0.4.1's).
- `scripts/argus-deploy.sh` reads the version back out of the journal after the
  restart; it must report `v0.4.2`, not `unknown` and not `v0.4.1`. That read-back
  is the only check that catches a silent reattach to the previous image.
- Re-run the EOD SOP against **2026-09-14** specifically —
  `python -m argus.tools.desk --config /etc/argus/argus.sandbox.toml --date 2026-09-14`
  — and confirm the row now reads `EXIT SPENT (...)` with a mark at **15:59:59**
  rather than 11:34:10, and that `EXITS THAT DID NOT CLOSE THE POSITION` lists the
  attempts. That day is the fix's own regression test against real data. **It does
  NOT expire** — the sandbox 24 h reset clears `positions` and `order_legs` and
  never `audit_log` (`store.py:750` (private record, not published)), and
  this tool reads the audit log alone. **What does change is the PROFILE**: after
  the cutover, `reconciliation.txt`'s `$PROFILE` resolves to `live` and the SOP
  reads `argus-live.db`, so any backward look at a cert date needs the explicit
  `--config /etc/argus/argus.sandbox.toml` above. That holds for the whole first
  live week, not just this check.
