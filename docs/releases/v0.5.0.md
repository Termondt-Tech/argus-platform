*The MINOR marks a change in how Argus manages a position, not in what size it
trades. Through v0.4.7 a spread Argus opened had exactly two ways out before the
close — a stop that fires when the trade goes wrong, and a clock that flattens
whatever is left at 15:30 — and it **never took a win**. This release adds the third
way: a **profit target**, a trigger that closes a spread the market has already paid
in full, and — because the flatten's normal outcome on a winning day is "no market,
so it expired" — a ledger that can now **value a trade held to expiry** instead of
leaving its result blank for ever. → [profit target and re-entry](../decisions/2026-09-22-profit-target-and-re-entry.md),
[valuing a held-to-expiry trade](../decisions/2026-09-22-valuing-a-held-to-expiry-trade.md)*

## Why a MINOR

[`README.md`](README.md#version-numbers) reserves the MINOR for a change of phase.
The rollout phases (spec §10: shadow → sandbox → live-min-size → scale) have not
changed — **Argus is still live at minimum size, and the size rails are untouched by
this release.** What changes is that Argus starts **optimizing its current strategy** — the same
strategy at the same size, managed better: until now the system only
defended a position (stop it out, flatten it); from here it also **harvests** one. A
profit target is a different kind of decision from a stop — it is the first exit that
fires because the trade *worked* — and it interacts with everything downstream of it:
the loss budget re-funds a second swing out of the first swing's profit, the re-entry
question becomes live, and a ledger that assumed every winner closes needed a way to
value one that does not. Richard's call, 2026-09-23, on the precedent of v0.4.0
(*"the MINOR marks that [a phase of work] is now the phase, not that the rollout
phase has changed"*).

**What that does and does not mean here:** the phase *begins* with this release
shipping the capability, and *starts trading it* when `profit_target_enabled` is
promoted to `true`. Until then the deployed behaviour is v0.4.7's, plus the ledger
valuation.

## Name

**Vindemiatrix** — ε Virginis, Latin *vindēmiātrix*, "the grape-harvestress"
(feminine of *vindēmiātor*, "grape-gatherer"; IAU-approved 2016). Its heliacal rising
— first visible before dawn after weeks of invisibility — told growers in antiquity
that the grapes were ripe and the vintage could begin, so the Greeks called it
*Protrygeter*, "the herald of the vintage." **It is the star you watch to know when
what you are holding is ready to be taken.** That is the profit target verbatim: the
trigger fires when a spread's mid rounds to one tick, the point at which everything
it was ever going to pay has been paid and nothing remains to collect but risk.

It earns the second half of the release too. The settlement valuation is the tally
after the harvest: a spread that expires unclosed had a result all along, and the
ledger now counts it instead of leaving the basket blank.

Found outside the pool — **Rastaban does not fit** (its reading is a second
independent validation loop that cross-checks a rail before a high-risk action fires;
this release adds no second loop, and the profit target is a plain trigger through the
existing exit path). Qualifies by tradition rather than literal "eye" etymology — a
star watched for a signal is a watcher's star, the standing already given to Alcor,
Arcturus and Kochab. Distinct from every spent name: Kochab (v0.4.7) is the reference
point *demoted to a guardian*; Arcturus (v0.4.5) is the guardian that keeps following;
none is about the moment of taking. **Confirmed against two independent sources**
(Wiktionary; Wikipedia's *Epsilon Virginis*, corroborated by BBC Sky at Night) before
being spent — the etymology and the heliacal-rising tradition are both well attested;
no second-source flag is owed. **Rastaban stays reserved; the pool is otherwise
empty.**

## What changed

### 1. The profit target — a fourth trigger, off by default

→ [decision §11–§12](../decisions/2026-09-22-profit-target-and-re-entry.md)

**Why it is close to free.** Argus never intends to hold to expiry: the 15:30
flatten pays the exit tick on every winner anyway. The choice is not *pay a tick*
against *pay nothing*, it is **pay a tick now against pay a tick in three hours while
holding full-width risk in between.** Measured over 5,010 live evaluations of a
nearly-worthless spread, the executable offer sat exactly one tick above the mid
91.7% of the time, at 12:00 exactly as at 15:30 — the tick does not decay while you
wait. Replayed over the 48 positions with a complete mark path: 20 reach the trigger,
18 of those the flatten would have paid the same tick (Δ $0), and **one** — a spread
that hit max profit at 11:38, bled back and stopped out at −$126 — is worth +$226.
**The cost side is the robust half and the benefit side is n=1**; that asymmetry is
the finding, and it is why this ships behind a switch.

- **`TriggerReason.PROFIT_TARGET`** fires on `round_to_tick(cost_to_close) ==
  TICK` — the cheapest *expressible* closing limit — debounced at the existing
  `watchdog.sustained_s = 5.0`. **It is not "mid ≤ 0.075"**: that fires at mid 0.00
  where `UnpriceableSpreadError` refuses to build the order, which is the 2026-09-22
  afternoon's `watchdog_exit_still_unplaced` every 30s on a *winning* position. A
  sweep over the whole 0.00–0.30 leg grid pins that the trigger is true exactly where
  the close builds at one tick. **An earlier draft triggered on `crossing_debit ≤
  0.05` and fired on 0 of 5,010 buildable ticks** — an arithmetic empty set, found
  from a live flatten that had been refusing for sixteen minutes while the record was
  being written (§10).
- **It never crosses.** A new `WalkPolicy.for_profit_target` is the third policy
  constructor beside entry and exit. It walks one tick, then two (a maximum debit of
  0.10, **pinned**, not read from config — §3 measured 0.15 and 0.20 as *worse than
  doing nothing*), and if nothing fills it does **not** escalate to the market-crossing
  limit. It cancels, confirms the order dead through `_await_settled`, and reports the
  new retryable `ExitOutcome.UNFILLED`. A profit take has no deadline that justifies
  paying for the outcomes that spend an emergency exit.
- **A failed take costs nothing.** It has its own `_profit_sends` / `_profit_spent`
  and a budget of two (`_PROFIT_SEND_ATTEMPTS`, a module constant on
  `_EXIT_SEND_ATTEMPTS`' precedent). **A take that does not fill never touches
  `_attempted`, `_sends` or `_flatten_attempted`** — an AST test sweeps every
  profit-path function for a write to, or a spend of, any of them. Exhausting the
  budget retires the *take* for that position and leaves the stop and the flatten
  override fully intact. A take that sent nothing (`NOT_ATTEMPTED`) is free and
  stands down 30s (`_PROFIT_REFUSAL_BACKOFF_S`) rather than re-firing every tick, so a
  broker outage cannot keep the position in `_exiting`, un-evaluated.
- **A take that FILLS spends the close, as any fill does** (2026-09-23 review). Its
  fill sits under the same `argus-close-*` key every later close would use, and
  `DuplicateOrderGuard` blocks on FILLED — so after a PARTIAL fill the stub could
  never be closed automatically, and until the review it was left believing its stop
  was intact. It now lands where an emergency fill's stub already lands: the
  repeating `watchdog_exit_spent_still_held` page with the live mark, and NO EXIT LEFT
  on `/status`. **The stub still needs a human** — a quantity-aware guard is a
  separate, OPEN decision ([closing the stub](../decisions/2026-09-23-closing-the-stub-a-partial-close-leaves.md)),
  and the recommended gate on promoting the switch.
- **A broker REJECTION of a take is paged once per position**
  (`watchdog_profit_target_rejected`): it is the broker refusing a CLOSE, which the
  stop will send too (C5).
- **It is the last resort of nothing.** Emergency triggers keep precedence; the take
  is acted on only when no emergency trigger fired and while the emergency exit is
  unspent. It can never be the reason a spread is held.
- **The switch.** `watchdog.profit_target_enabled` is a strict bool (`1` and
  `"false"` are refused at boot). No tri-state "observe" mode: the trigger is a pure
  function of `cost_to_close`, which `watchdog.check` already records at 1 Hz, so
  *"would it have fired, and when"* is computable from next week's live rows — a
  shadow week would cost a deploy and buy nothing.
- **`/status` has a Profit target row** showing the *running* switch (the only place a
  config promotion can be confirmed live), takes this session, and each held position
  the take has retired — amber only when its order may still be working, never red.
- **Paging.** A take pages when it closes the position (the executor's usual
  `watchdog_exit_filled`) and when it retires *unconfirmed*
  (`watchdog_profit_target_retired`, `order_may_be_working: true`). It does **not**
  page when unfilled, when its budget runs out with nothing at the broker, or when it
  cannot be built. It does not send `watchdog_triggered`, the stop-loss page.
- **One hazard the build found.** Sharing the `_TRIGGERED` edge would have let an
  unfilled take swallow a *stop's* page: the edge stays risen, and a backstop breach
  the next tick arrives as Tier 2 `triggered_still` with nothing on the phone. The
  take has its own `_PROFIT_TRIGGERED` tag, pinned by the sequence test.

**The re-entry half is deliberately NOT built, and not coupled.** Re-entry is already
automatic; shipping the target ships more of it, because the target fires about an
hour earlier (10:34 against 11:47 on 2026-09-01). And it interacts with the loss
budget: `remaining` is unclamped, so after a take banks +$90 the next candidate sizes
to a full 2 lots — **the profit target mechanically funds a full-size second swing
out of the first swing's profit.** `signal.entry_latest` is untouched (a 15:00
entry against a 15:30 flatten permits a 31-minute trade; lowering it is a config-only
lever that moves and measures independently). Shipping the two together would make
the next bad day unattributable to either.

### 2. A held-to-expiry trade is valued at settlement in the ledger

→ [decision](../decisions/2026-09-22-valuing-a-held-to-expiry-trade.md)

The ledger's first live night found the hole tests could not: on 2026-09-22 the 15:30
flatten found no market, refused to invent a price, and the put spread expired
worthless. The ledger read the day as `PROVISIONAL` with a blank gross — honest, and
**permanent**, because an OTM expiry produces no fill and no broker transaction to
fill the blank. The Dashboard's headline Net P&L would have silently excluded a
+$93.12 day, every time the flatten cannot find a market — which is its normal
outcome on a winning day.

- **`tools/settlement.py`** values the trade through `shadow.outcome`'s own math
  (`settlement_liability`, `classify` — an AST test forbids re-deriving it) from the
  **day's last recorded spot** (`watchdog.check` and `check_spent`, newest by `seq`),
  or from a per-host `/etc/argus/settlements.csv` official print, which wins and
  **persists** (the ledger rebuilds every night, so a one-off value would be
  overwritten). No network: any past day re-renders to the identical number.
- **It is a proxy, and every row says so** — `gross_basis` ∈ `fills` |
  `settlement_spot` | `settlement_official` and four refusals (`spot_too_early`, more
  than 15 minutes before the close; `no_spot`; `settlement_file_error`; blank for an
  unpriceable execution). **Unknown stays blank, never zero.**
- **The broker comparison stays FILLS ONLY — the load-bearing rule.** The broker has
  no row for an OTM expiry, so a settlement number in that comparison would make every
  such day `DIFF` for a difference that is correct. As built, `days.gross` is **fill
  cash** (the recon input), `days.total_gross` is the day's result, and the Dashboard
  sums `total_gross`. `PROVISIONAL` regains its meaning: a trade valued by neither
  fills nor settlement.
- **A per-trade invariant replaces the check that moved.** Once the recon's Argus side
  became cash, nothing compared the P&L with anything; reproduced with a short leg
  filled 2 against a long leg filled 1, a $120 error reconciled `OK`. Now
  `gross == cash the trade's own fills moved − what its expired contracts owed`, to the
  cent; a failure fills `trades.pnl_check`, names the trade in `recon.pnl_mismatch` and
  makes the day `DIFF` — even with the broker unread.
- **Dashboard.** Net P&L sums `total_gross`; a new KPI, "Of which valued at
  settlement"; `LAST 20 TRADES` gains the basis. Column letters are now **derived**
  from the row dataclasses' field order (every pre-existing letter came out
  identical, and a test pins them); the new columns are appended at the end.
- **`argus-eod.sh`** mounts the settlements file read-only only if it exists, and
  warns on the report channel when it is not readable by uid 10001 — the script runs
  as root, so `-r` proved nothing.
- **One thing still open, on purpose.** An ITM expiry *does* post a broker cash
  settlement whose `transaction_sub_type` has never been seen. Today it makes the day
  `DIFF`, loudly, which is the designed answer and will look like a defect. **Do not
  guess the strings or the row's date in advance**; capture the first one as a
  fixture, then pair it with the settlement-valued trade.

### 3. The "budget exhausted" page no longer fires on an ordinary day

`5c4a617`, `src/` only, no config key. The once-a-day `daily_loss.budget_exhausted`
page fired whenever *any* budget refusal was on a measured budget — including on
**every day Argus held a full-size position**: a 2-lot 5-wide at a 0.50 credit commits
900 of the 950 budget, so the next candidate five seconds later was refused and paged
"exhausted" when the budget was merely tied up in the open spread (2026-09-22, 10:00
ET). The page now asks the gate's own question again *as if nothing were open*, and
fires only when the day's own losses have used the budget. `gate.decision` still
records every refusal with its numbers; the gate's *verdicts* are unchanged.

## A visible telemetry change for everyone, switch on or off

The profit-target trigger is **always evaluated**, so from the first tick after
deploy:

- `watchdog.check` carries `profit_target` in `true_reasons` whenever it would fire —
  the replay input the design wanted;
- every degraded/impaired record and `/status` row lists
  `unknown_reasons: [credit_multiple, profit_target]` where it used to list
  `credit_multiple` alone. **The backstop is live in exactly the same cases**; the list
  is longer because two triggers now read the mid;
- a spent position's `still_triggered` reads emergency reasons only.

Anyone diffing `/status` or the check rows against v0.4.7 should expect this and not
read it as a regression.

## Why it's safe

- **Shipped off.** Deploying the image changes no order Argus sends. With the switch
  at `false` there is no outbound call from the profit path, asserted structurally on
  `shadow.would_submit`'s shape.
- **The emergency exit and the flatten are untouched unless the take fills.** A take
  that does not fill cannot write or spend `_attempted`, `_sends` or
  `_flatten_attempted` (AST-pinned), and a crashed take retires *the take* — in memory
  first — and still halts entries. A take that fills spends the close exactly as an
  emergency fill does, because the guard would block any later close anyway.
- **The take never crosses and never invents a price.** Two ticks is the floor for
  every legal `exit_walk_away_pct`; the executor refuses to walk a take from any
  opening limit but one tick (`NOT_ATTEMPTED`, free).
- **No new way to double-close.** The emergency send already runs
  `guard_first_submission=True`, so a profit order somehow still working makes it
  return `BLOCKED` rather than close twice. The one case that cannot be made retryable
  — a cancel that is never confirmed (C5) — retires the take and pages with
  `order_may_be_working: true`.
- **No size rail moved.** `max_concurrent_positions`, `max_loss_per_position`,
  `daily_max_loss`, the loss budget and the welded invariant are untouched.
- **The ledger side is read-only**, `mode=ro`, no writer lock, no market-data
  session, no submission-path import — as v0.4.7.
- **No migration, no new audit category.**

## What this release does NOT do

- **Does not turn the profit target on.** That is a config flip, on its own day.
- **Does not build or gate the re-entry**, and does not touch `signal.entry_latest`.
  The re-entry gate needs capture-rate-by-hold-time on live fills at an effective n
  above ~40 days — roughly two more months of live sessions.
- **Does not change the stop, the flatten, the walk-away parameters or any size
  rail.**
- **Does not pair an ITM broker settlement row with its trade** — it stays `DIFF`
  until a real one is captured as a fixture.
- **Does not read the official Cboe settlement print automatically.** The recorded
  spot is a proxy; the persistent override file is the correction path.
