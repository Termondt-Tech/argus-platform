*Argus has recorded the **mid** of every leg it ever selected and never once the
**spread**. So the audit log could say what Argus thought a spread was worth, and

## What changed

### 1. The quoted spread is recorded, entry side

**Raw `bid` and `ask` per leg, on both entry records:**

| Record | Tier | Where the fields sit |
|---|---|---|
| `signal.candidate` | 1 | nested, beside the existing `mid`: `short.bid`, `short.ask`, `long.bid`, `long.ask` |
| `signal.none` near-miss | 2 | flat, beside the existing `short_strike`: `per_side.<side>.short_bid` / `short_ask` / `long_bid` / `long_ask` |

The shapes differ on purpose — match the record you're joining, not the other record.

**Raw, not a derived half-spread**, for two reasons a width can't carry. `bid == 0` is
the only way the zero-bid floor's firing becomes visible at all: `floor_bid` quietly
turns a zero bid into a tick, so the recorded credit is systematically optimistic
relative to a crossing exit, by exactly the floor. And *which* leg is the wide one is a
selection question that bears on `target_short_delta` and the long-leg search. Every
derived quantity follows from `(bid, ask)`; the reverse is false. `mid` stays, even
though it's now redundant — `marker._decode` and `tools.desk` read it back.

**On the rejected tick too, and that is the half that makes the dataset usable.**
`min_credit` rejects on credit, credit is a difference of floored mids, and a floored
mid is a function of the very spread being measured. Recording the spread only where
the credit cleared would sample the tail of its own distribution.

### 2. The quoted spread is recorded, exit side

`crossing_debit` — the short ask minus the long bid, the price a market-crossing limit
actually fills at — rides `TriggerEvaluation.to_payload()` beside `cost_to_close`. That
method is splatted into `watchdog.check`, `watchdog.check_spent`,
`_report_evaluability`'s pages and `watchdog.triggered`, so one field reaches all four.

**One field, not four leg prices**, because of an identity:
`crossing_debit − cost_to_close` is exactly the sum of the two half-spreads, which is the
whole friction quantity for a two-leg structure. It is exact only where both legs are
two-sided — `cost_to_close` floors a zero bid and `crossing_debit` deliberately does not,
so a zero long bid adds 0.025. Analysis conditions on that, which is what the entry
side's raw bids are for.

*v0.4.3 had already shipped this field.* v0.4.4 routes it through the shared guard
below, and the two changes are reconciled — see §6.

### 3. One payload guard

`signal.models.payload_number()` is now **the** rule for a number on its way into the
audit log: non-finite becomes `None`. It had been written four different ways
(`_payload_delta`, `_payload_age`, the `underlying_price` ternary and
`strategy._num`), all behaviour-identical, and a test now asserts that rather than
trusting it. The precedent is `zerobid`'s own: *two implementations of the same safety
rule is how one of them quietly drifts.*

**`risk/gate.py::_num` is deliberately not collapsed into it.** It stringifies where
this nulls, because in a gate decision an `inf` (a never-connected stream) is the
information, not noise to erase.

### 4. A latent defect closed: `cost_to_close` reached the payload unguarded

`evaluate_triggers` already marked a non-finite `cost_to_close` as a problem and made
`CREDIT_MULTIPLE` unknown — and then `to_payload` recorded the raw value anyway. So in
the one branch where the number was known to be unusable, the record that would
explain the refusal was the record that got dropped. Every numeric in that method now
goes through `payload_number`. Unreachable through the live DXLink adapter, which maps
non-finite prices to `0.0` before they get here; reachable through any other port.

**Why the guard matters more than it looks.** A refused Tier 1 write raises
`ValueError`, not `AuditWriteError`, and `SignalLoop.run` swallows it as "a bad
iteration". `signal.candidate` is written *above* the gate and the submit. So before
this, one non-finite field on one leg would have **silently stopped every entry** —
no alert, no `/status` row, one `logger.exception` per tick. A telemetry field would
have become a trading-behaviour change. `test_a_hostile_quote_does_not_silently_suppress_every_entry`
drives exactly that case end to end and asserts the candidate still reaches the gate.

### 5. The last raw numeric on an entry record: `signal.none`'s spot

Found while drafting these notes. `orchestrator.evaluate_once` wrote
`chain.underlying_price` onto `signal.none` directly — one file outside the three this
change's guard was scoped to — and it now goes through `payload_number` like every
other number.

**Not reachable from the live feed**: `dxlink._to_float` maps a NaN or inf price to
`None` and then `0.0`, so every live `Quote` is finite. **Reachable from any other
port**: `FakeMarketData` hands raw floats to the book unchecked, `Quote.mid` lets NaN
through (it returns `None` only on `ask <= 0.0`, and a comparison with NaN is `False`),
and `live_chain.snapshot()` checks only `spot is None`. The harm was bounded — Tier 2
would drop the whole `signal.none` row, taking that tick's near miss and its quoted
spread with it — and **nothing decides on this value**: its only decision consumer, the
entry drift breaker, already fails closed on a non-finite reference.

Fixed at the payload boundary rather than in `Quote.mid`, on the design's own rule that
the guard belongs to the boundary and not to whichever adapter is installed. Making
`Quote.mid` refuse non-finite would change behaviour (`snapshot()` would skip the tick)
for every reader of it.

### 6. Reconciling with v0.4.3

This work was built on its own branch before v0.4.3 shipped, and both added
`crossing_debit` to `TriggerEvaluation.to_payload()`. Resolved at the merge:

- **The branch's payload won** — same key, same semantics, every field guarded.
- **v0.4.3's `TriggerEvaluation.crossing_debit` property now delegates to
  `payload_number`.** It had its own inline `is_finite` check, a fourth spelling of the
  rule. The one-definition AST test only recognises single-`return` wrappers, and that
  property had three statements, so it would have slipped past the test. `to_payload`
  reads the property, so the expression exists once.
- **`watchdog.exit_started` KEEPS `crossing_debit`, now through `payload_number`.**
  The merge first removed v0.4.3's copy in favour of joining to the triggering row
  (`_trigger` writes the evaluation payload before handing the same `market` to the
  exit). **Review on 2026-09-18 restored it**, because that join is exact only on the
  trigger's rising edge, where the row is Tier 1 `watchdog.triggered`. Every later exit
  on a still-triggered position (the flatten on a spent position, the
  `NOTHING_AT_BROKER` / `CROSS_UNPRICEABLE` retries, the flatten's override) follows a
  Tier 2 `watchdog.triggered_still`, which is queued, so its `seq` can land after the
  Tier 1 `exit_started`, and dropped when the queue is full. Those are the flatten-regime
  exits the owed `exit_walk_away_pct` re-derivation needs. The copy is read off the very
  `market` the walk was handed, so it cannot disagree with the triggering row. The AST
  test allows exactly that one path-qualified site. Pinned by
  `test_exit_started_carries_the_walk_range_AND_the_offer`. **The v0.4.3 → v0.4.4
  history is continuous: both versions write the key.**

### 7. The EOD report runs itself

Since the cert era the end-of-day read has been four commands typed by hand at
~16:30 ET. In live that read is the operator's only daily look at a real account, it
gained a step (**G1** — the daily-loss breaker's input) that `/status` can no longer
answer after the close, and a manual SOP fails silently: a skipped evening looks like
a quiet one. → [the decision record](../decisions/2026-09-17-automated-eod-report.md)

- **`python -m argus.tools.eod`** (new, `src/`) renders every section in one
  container start: flat check, G1, rails (`/status`), `tools.desk`, a `tools.mark
  --dry-run --from-feed` scoring preview, `tools.evaluability`, a journal sweep, the
  nightly snapshot's age, and `tools.regime` on the week's last trading day. It
  writes a `.txt` transcript, a `.json` digest and a ready-to-POST `.slack.json`.
- **`scripts/argus-eod.sh` + `argus-eod.{service,timer}`** (host, 16:45 ET) handle
  the plumbing only: profile guard, journal grep, the `docker compose run`, delivery,
  90-day retention in `/path/to/backups/reports/` (inside the path the NAS key can
  already read).
- **The NAS collects the transcripts.** `scripts/argus-pull.sh` (the script installed
  on the NAS) gained a second, independent rsync arm, `/reports/argus-eod-*` →
  `/path/to/nas/argus-pull/reports/`. It uses the same key and the same rrsync
  root, with no `authorized_keys` change, and a reports failure never costs the
  snapshot pull. A wiring test pins the arm and that the report directory stays
  inside the key's root. `pull-snapshots.example.sh` got the same line.
  **`argus-pull-check` still cannot see this arm fail on its own** (one key, so it
  sees logins, not files); that shows in the NAS's `pull.log`.
- **G1 comes from `gate.decision` rows, not `/status`.** The session clears the risk
  source on the way out, so at 16:45 `/status` has no `nlv` and no `daily_pnl`. The
  rows give the day's *path*. **A day with no candidates says "no series", never
  `0.00`** — a zero shown as a number is the exact failure G1 exists to catch.
  Day 1 (2026-09-16, FOMC blackout) is that shape. **A row with no account state is
  excluded, not read as zero** (review fix, 2026-09-18): the snapshot builder writes
  `nlv 0.0` / `daily_pnl 0.0` as placeholders when it has no account, and one such row
  beside a stuck day P&L would have read as "MOVED". Such rows are counted and named
  (`g1.no_account_rows`, and on the Slack G1 line).
- **The journal sweep is scoped to the report's date** (review fix, 2026-09-18). The
  script still collects 36 h, so a next-morning catch-up covers the owed session, but
  now asks `journalctl -o short-iso` for timestamps and `tools.eod` keeps only lines
  whose ET date is the report's. Before, every punctual report repeated the previous
  day's hits and called them today's. Lines with no readable timestamp are kept and
  counted; the transcript names how many other-date lines were left out.
- **A composed tool can no longer keep the process's logging** (review fix,
  2026-09-18). `tools.mark.main` calls `logging.basicConfig` inside `_capture`, which
  bound a root handler to the scoring section's buffer, so later sections' log lines
  vanished into it. `_capture` now restores root handlers and logger levels.
- **`desk.summarize()` was extracted, not duplicated.** The digest reads the same
  object `tools.desk` prints, so there is one set of numbers for the day, not two.
  `tools.desk`'s output is unchanged.
- **A failed section still ships the report.** Its error goes into the transcript in
  place and is named in the digest. Only failing to produce or deliver the report at
  all fails the unit, and that pages on the **alert** webhook.
- **Which days an undated run reports: every settled session since the newest report
  on disk** (review fix, 2026-09-18 — it was "the latest settled session", so an
  outage across two sessions reported the second and silently skipped the first). It
  renders each day once, oldest first, up to `tools.eod.CATCH_UP_DAYS` (7) back;
  older unreported sessions are named in the journal, not rendered. With no report on
  disk (a fresh install) it is the latest session alone. A Saturday run reports
  Friday. `ARGUS_EOD_DATE` always re-renders. The transcript is written LAST, via a
  rename, because it is the render-once key. **An explicit date that has not settled
  yet is PRINTED, never written to `--out-dir`** (re-review fix, 2026-09-18): a
  mid-session preview (`ARGUS_EOD_DATE=<today>` at 14:00) used to become the day's
  report, and the 16:45 run then found nothing owed. The preview still prints to the
  journal; the punctual report is still owed.
- **A failed Slack POST is retried** (review fix, 2026-09-18). It used to be final: the
  transcript existed, so render-once made every later run a no-op. The script now
  touches `argus-eod-<date>.delivered` only after a successful POST, and re-posts any
  unmarked report from the last 3 days (`ARGUS_REPORT_RETRY_DAYS`) on the next run —
  `sudo systemctl start argus-eod`, or tomorrow's timer — without re-rendering. A
  report written by the current run always posts. The journal is now collected over 8
  days to cover the catch-up window.
- **A failed shared read costs its dependants, not the report** (review fix,
  2026-09-18). The ledger, G1, blackout and unwatchable reads used to run outside the
  per-section guard, so one exception failed the whole run. Each is now guarded: a
  failure is named first in the transcript (`READS THAT FAILED`) and in the digest's
  `failures`, and every value that depends on it reads `null`/UNREAD — never empty or
  zero. An unread ledger is the `FLAT STATE UNKNOWN` headline.
- **Its own channel.** `ARGUS_REPORT_SLACK_WEBHOOK_URL` (`#argus-eod`). If it is
  unset the digest falls back to the alert webhook; with neither, the report is
  still written to disk.

**Reviewed 2026-09-18, and four defects fixed before it shipped:** an overnight
catch-up reported the wrong day and could skip the missed one; a spread held to expiry
paged as `NOT FLAT — OPEN` in red at 16:45 although it had already settled (it is now
`:warning: HELD TO EXPIRY` — still the headline, since nothing closed it); a failed
Slack POST exited 0; and the Slack line named the container's `/reports` path instead
of the host path. The decision record has the detail.

**And a test-suite incident, fixed here.** `tools.eod.main()` calls `load_env()`,
which writes the repo's `.env` into `os.environ` outside pytest's monkeypatch. The
first tests to drive a tool's `main()` therefore leaked the real alert webhook into
the rest of the run, and every startup test after them **posted to Slack**.
`tests/conftest.py` now blinds `.env` loading and clears the webhook variables for
every test. A test pins that, and the EOD tests stub `tools.mark` so none of them can
reach the live feed.

**What it must never do** (pinned by `tests/test_deploy_wiring.py`): run a
write-mode `tools.mark`, take the writer lock, stop or restart `argus.service`, name
a profile or database by literal, or default the profile.

### 8. `/status`'s per-hour rates are a trailing slope, not `since-start / uptime`

Closes [the 2026-09-01 record](../decisions/2026-09-01-status-per-hour-rates-startup-contaminated.md),
which was diagnosed and never fixed. `rss_growth_bytes_per_hour` and
`gc_gen2_per_hour` divided a **fixed** startup cost (~5 MB of RSS, a gen-2 sweep or
two before the first `/status` is served) by uptime. That gives a bias,
`startup / uptime`, which decays and never goes away. On the v0.3.14 boot they read
**43 MB/h and 16.1 gen2/h at uptime 447 s**, on a process whose RSS and gen-2 count
had not moved since 81 s. The sustained figures were 1.5 MB/h and 0.039/h. The old
120 s floor (`_MIN_UPTIME_FOR_RATE_S`) could not separate the two, because the bias
is continuous and a cutoff is a step.

- **Now:** `status.TrailingRates` measures the slope over the last hour
  (`RATE_WINDOW_S = 3600`). A sampler task feeds it every 60 s, started beside the
  heartbeat and stopped in `_teardown`. The anchor is the newest sample at least a
  window old, and the history stays at about 62 samples however long the process
  lives. It is not seeded at construction, because that is where the startup cost
  starts. **The window is measured on the monotonic clock**, not the wall clock, so
  an NTP step cannot skew it, and **a failed sample no longer kills the sampler**
  — it is logged and the next tick runs (both review fixes, 2026-09-18).
- **For the first hour after any start, both rates are `null`.** That is correct and
  expected. The absolute `rss_growth_bytes` is still there from the first poll.
- **JSON:** same two keys, which now mean *per hour over the trailing
  `rate_window_s`*. `rate_window_s` is new, and reads just over 3600 once
  populated. **HTML:** `… MB/h over last 60 min`, and gen-2 prints to two decimals,
  so 0.039/h no longer shows as `0.0`.
- **Pinned by** `tests/test_status_rates.py`. The test replays the incident: `null`
  at 447 s, `0.0` on a flat process at 2 h (the old formula printed 2.6 MB/h), and a
  1.5 MB/h leak measured at 1.5 MB/h under the same startup bump. It also covers
  bounded history, a repeated instant, missing `/proc`, the sampler's lifecycle,
  a wall-clock step that leaves the rate unmoved, and a failed sample the sampler
  survives. **An AST test fails if anything in `status.py` divides by `uptime_s`
  again.**

Nothing reads either field: no rail, no alert, no `tools.*`. **Finding 7's question
is still open** — is the ~1.5 MB/h ramp a leak, or garbage waiting for collection?
This makes the field fit to answer it; it does not answer it.

### 9. `/status` says when a day is blacked out

→ [the decision record](../decisions/2026-09-18-status-says-when-a-day-is-blacked-out.md)

**The finding.** At 08:45 ET on 2026-09-11 (CPI), `/status` read `event_blackout.state:
ok` and `next_session_open: 09:40`. Nothing said the session would take no entries. The
row described the **file**, meaning its horizon and date count, and
`read_blackout_runway` loaded the full set of dates and then discarded it. The audit
trail was silent too: the strategy checks the entry window *before* the blackout, so
every `signal.none` until 10:00 said `outside_entry_window`. Live day 1 (2026-09-16,
FOMC) had the same shape, and on a blackout day a legitimate live `daily_pnl: 0.0`
looked no different from a broken day.

**The page (`status.py`, `app.py`).**

- `BlackoutRunway` keeps the date set. `covers(day)` returns `False` when the
  blackout is disabled and `None` when the file is unreadable, so an unreadable
  file never renders as an ordinary day.
- **JSON:** two new keys in the existing `event_blackout` block,
  `today_blacked_out` and `next_session_blacked_out`. `state` still reads `ok`,
  because it describes the file.
- **HTML:** three additions.
  - A grey `Entries: NONE TODAY — event blackout` row under the phase, where the
    entries-ended stand-down already renders.
  - A grey `IN FORCE TODAY` badge leading the Event blackout row.
  - A suffix on **Next session open** (`— event blackout: no entries that day`)
    when *that* session's date is blacked out. This matters because
    `next_session_open` is tomorrow's open while a session runs.
- **Grey, never amber.** Nothing is wrong and there is nothing to do. An ordinary
  day adds nothing to the page.

**`next_session_open` is unchanged, on purpose.** A blackout day still opens a session:
reconcile, the watchdog, exits and the pre-close flatten all run. Skipping blackout days
in that field would misreport when the process wakes. The new flag says what the
session will not do, beside the field that says when it starts.

**The session's half (`session.py`).** At session start, `_announce_blackout_day`
prints `event blackout: <date> IS A BLACKOUT DAY — no entries will be taken; …`. It
also writes one **Tier 1 `session.event_blackout`** row (`{trading_date}`), but only on
a blackout day, because the row's presence is the answer. It reads **the same
frozenset the strategy is built from**, and an AST test pins that, so this is the
session's own verdict and independent of `/status`'s startup read of the file. The
strategy's check order and the blackout rail itself are untouched. Nothing reads the
row.

**One caveat.** `/status` reads the file once, at process start. For a statement
about *today* that is fine only because `blackout_dates.txt` is a bind mount: the
deploy restarts on any change to it, and there is no hot-reload. A hand edit without a
restart would show up as the page and the session-start record disagreeing.

**The EOD report names it too (`tools/eod.py`).** Live day 1's digest said `no series
(no candidates evaluated against the account)`, which was true and could not be told
apart from a broken signal path. `read_blackout_day` reads the **report date's**
blackout **off the chain**, from `session.event_blackout` or from `signal.none` rows
with reason `event_blackout`. The second source covers every blackout day there has
ever been, including 2026-09-16, which predates the session-start row.

The blackout does not come from `/status`: `today_blacked_out` answers for the moment
`/status` was polled, and the report may be a next-morning catch-up or a `--date`
re-render. It does not come from `blackout_dates.txt` either, because a regenerated
file drops past dates.

- **Transcript:** an `EVENT BLACKOUT` line in SUBJECT, and G1's "no series" now says
  the outcome was designed.
- **Digest JSON:** a new `blackout_day` block, plus `rails.blackout_next_session` and
  `rails.next_session_open`.
- **Slack line:** `entries … — EVENT BLACKOUT, no entries by design`, and
  `G1 no series (event blackout — …; G1 still unread)`.
- **The next session is the one blackout fact read from `/status`**, because it is
  about the future. It shows as a `next session` line in RAILS, and as a `next` line in
  the digest when tomorrow is blacked out. That line is a heads-up, not an alarm, and
  it is absent on an ordinary evening.
- **The one blackout alarm:** an entry attempt on a blackout day, which by design
  means the strategy's rail did not hold.
- `tools.eod` spells `session.event_blackout` rather than importing `argus.session`,
  which would load the broker SDK. A test pins that the two spellings agree.

**Not in this release:** a "next entry day" field (part B of the 2026-09-11
assessment).

### 10. The day P&L says whether it is anchored

Found in the 2026-09-18 code review. `DailyPnl.value()` returns `0.0` until the day
has an anchor, and nothing recorded which kind of zero it was — so an unanchored day
P&L was indistinguishable from a flat day on `gate.decision`, on `/status`, and in
the EOD report's G1 read. That is the G1 failure in its exact form: the breaker reads
the placeholder and cannot trip.

- **`RiskSnapshot.daily_pnl_anchored`** (new, `bool | None`, default `None` = "the
  caller did not say"). `RiskSnapshotBuilder` sets it from `DailyPnl.anchored` — and
  to `False` in its no-account branch, whose `0.0` is a placeholder whatever the
  anchor says (re-review fix: an anchored day with no account state used to render a
  green `$0.00` as a reading).
- **`gate.decision`** carries it in `snapshot.daily_pnl_anchored` (Tier 1, a key in
  the existing blob).
- **`/status`:** `account.daily_pnl_anchored` is new, and **`account.daily_pnl` reads
  `null` — not `0.0` — when it is `false`**. The HTML Day P&L row shows an amber
  `UNANCHORED` badge. The runbook's G1 cross-check reads this field, so a
  placeholder must not look like a reading there either.
- **`tools.eod`'s G1** drops unanchored day-P&L values from the series (the NLV on
  the same row is real and is kept), counts them (`g1.unanchored_rows`, and on the
  Slack line), and reports a day with an NLV path but no anchored P&L as **NO
  READINGS** rather than "one value all session". A row written before this key
  existed has no flag and reads as it always did.
- **The session says so at start** (re-review fix). The anchor is attempted once,
  at session start; if it neither anchors nor restores, the journal now carries
  `DAY P&L NOT ANCHORED: … CANNOT TRIP` — it used to say nothing. The EOD journal
  sweep and the cheatsheet's morning grep both match that line. Re-trying the anchor
  later in the day would move the rail's baseline, so it is not done here.
- **Decides nothing.** `daily_pnl` itself is unchanged, and the gate and breaker read
  it exactly as before. Making an unanchored day refuse entries would be a rail
  change, and it is not in this release.

## Why it's safe

- **Nothing reads the new fields.** Every value is written and never consulted — no
  comparison, no branch, no threshold. That is an AST test, not a review opinion, and
  it is what makes "no behaviour change" a structural claim.
- **No schema change.** The audit payload is one JSON `TEXT` column, so these are keys
  in a blob, not columns. No migration file, `PRAGMA user_version` doesn't move, and a
  database holding rows from before and after this deploy is the expected state.
- **Readers are unbroken.** `marker._decode` and `tools.desk` read named keys and
  ignore the rest; both are tested against payloads carrying the new keys.
- **Latency is negligible, and it was measured rather than assumed.** `signal.candidate`
  is Tier 1, awaited on the signal loop. The added on-loop serialize-and-hash cost is
  **+1.77 µs** once per 5 s, against a 50,000 µs loop-lag alarm. The Tier 2 records cost
  the producing loops nothing — serialization happens in the consumer task. The fsync
  dominates a Tier 1 write and is unchanged.
- **Growth is under 1 MB/day** — bytes added to existing rows, not new rows.
- **The data is admissible in cert** too. `[marketdata] source = "live"` everywhere, and
  a quoted spread is a quote fact; nothing here touches a fill.
- **The EOD report is a separate process that only reads.** It is a one-shot
  container with `mode=ro` reads and no lock, and nothing in it depends on
  `argus.service`. The trading process imports nothing from `tools.eod`, and
  `tools.desk`'s output is unchanged by the `summarize()` extraction.
  Until the timer is enabled, nothing runs it.
- **The rate fix is diagnostic-only.** It changes two derived `/status` numbers and
  adds one. The sampler is one `statm` read plus `gc.get_stats()` a minute on the
  loop. Both are O(1), and neither is `gc.get_objects()`, which an existing AST test
  forbids. Nothing decides on any of it.
- **The blackout fix touches no rail.** The strategy's blackout check, and its
  position after the entry-window check, are unchanged. The page adds two JSON keys
  and three HTML additions, derived from a set the process already loaded. The
  session adds at most one Tier 1 write per blackout day, at session start and
  before the signal loop starts. A failed write halts like every other Tier 1 write,
  which is the audit log's uniform rule rather than a new risk. Nothing reads the
  row.
- **The anchored flag (§10) changes no decision.** It is one boolean on the risk
  snapshot, recorded on `gate.decision` and shown on `/status`; the gate and the
  breaker read `daily_pnl` exactly as before. The one visible change is `/status`'s
  `account.daily_pnl` reading `null` instead of a placeholder `0.0` while unanchored.

## What this release does NOT do

- **It gates nothing.** No `PreTradeGate` input, no strategy input, no trigger, no size
  effect, no alert, no threshold, and no `/status` row for the quoted spread (§8
  changes two existing `/status` numbers and adds `rate_window_s`, all diagnostic;
  §9 adds blackout visibility and leaves the blackout rail itself untouched). Acting on a spread width needs a
  number to set a threshold from, and producing that number is what this release is for.
- **It does not change what `credit` means.** The zero-bid floor stays; this only makes
  its firing visible.
- **It records no displayed depth.** Argus trades through a complex-order auction, so
  per-leg top-of-book depth doesn't describe the liquidity it actually accesses.
- **It does not answer whether friction eats the edge.** It's the instrument; that
  measurement needs live fills.
- **The two exit-walk telemetry gaps stay parked**: the market state at a failed cross
  (`_cross_limit` collapses three causes into one `None` and discards the debit) and the
  mark going dark for the 4–23 s an exit walk runs. Reopen them when a live cross fails
  and the record can't say what the market looked like.
- **Finding 13** (credit at fixed delta by minute of day) is pure analysis over data
  that already exists and needs no deploy.
