# v0.3.13 — Tejat

*TAGGED and DEPLOYED 2026-08-30 (Sunday); the image running the 2026-08-31 cert
live-fire session. `watchdog.unmanaged_order_cancel` shipped `false` — P5's cancel
recheck is in `would_cancel` mode. Named per the Argus's Eyes convention
(`docs/releases/README.md`) — μ Geminorum (Tejat Posterior), from Arabic
*Al-Tahāyī*, "The Eye" of the Twin in Gemini. Spent from the pool, and its own
waiting reading — "state-machine disambiguation... a case where identical market
structure produced two conflicting interpretations" — names this release's
dominant fix almost exactly: `WalkOutcome.UNRESOLVED` was one value standing for
two opposite epistemic states, "nobody asked the broker" and "the broker was asked
and said nothing exists," and splitting it is what let a confirmed-empty answer
retry instead of spending a triggered spread's only exit. The release's other two
legs — pricing a spent position instead of going dark, and a clock that needs no
market at all — are not the disambiguation itself, but both exist because the
watchdog had, until this release, only one way to read "the exit did not work."

From the 2026-08-28 exit-attempt-taxonomy pass: an intraday
cert outage put the exit path into the worst state it can reach — a credit stop
fired, the closing order was refused by an unreadable broker, and the position sat
held and triggered for 76 minutes with nothing automated able to try again and no
surface able to say what it was worth. Three fixes from that pass, all landing here:
a broker-confirmed "nothing exists" is no longer treated the same as "nobody looked";
a spent exit attempt is priced on every tick instead of going dark; and a 0DTE
position now has a control that needs no market at all.*

**Requires an image rebuild AND a config update, and they must deploy together.**
`config.py`, `execution/loop.py`, `execution/entry.py`, `execution/watchdog.py`,
`execution/working_orders.py` (new), `execution/residual_orders.py` (new),
`execution/escalation.py` (new — `_Escalation` lifted out of `watchdog.py`
unchanged, so the new module can share the one implementation without closing an
import ring), `execution/fake.py`, `marketcalendar.py`, `risk/models.py`,
`risk/snapshot.py`, `risk/gate.py`, `reconcile.py`, `app.py`, `session.py`,
`status.py` all change. `argus.toml`/`argus.dev.toml` gain **two** new **required**
keys — `watchdog.flatten_before_close_s` (P2) and `watchdog.unmanaged_order_max_s`
(P5) — plus one optional, defaulting `watchdog.unmanaged_order_cancel`. An old
config against the new image refuses to start (`missing required config key
watchdog.flatten_before_close_s`), and the new config's values mean nothing to the
old image. Same coupling `[reconcile]` (v0.3.11) and `staleness.greeks_max_age_s`
(v0.3.12) already needed; the second required key rides the cutover the first one
already pays for.

## Fixed

**A broker-confirmed "nothing exists" was treated the same as "nobody looked," and
it cost a triggered spread its only exit.** `WalkOutcome.UNRESOLVED` was produced
from two sites with opposite epistemic content: one where the broker was genuinely
unreachable, and one where a submit failed and the broker, asked under that
identifier, positively answered that nothing worked and nothing filled. The exit
path's own one-attempt rule reasons that a retry "would stack onto something we
cannot see" — true for the first case, **false for the second**, since there is
nothing to see. On 2026-08-28 the second case is what fired: a cert 502 during an
exit send, the broker's read confirming empty, and the position's one exit attempt
spent on that confirmed-empty answer, 76 minutes before anyone could look at it
again. **Fixed** with `WalkOutcome.NO_ORDER_EXISTS`, a new outcome distinct from
`UNRESOLVED`, mapped by `ExitExecutor._after_walk` to `ExitOutcome.NOTHING_AT_BROKER`
ahead of the `is not WALKED_AWAY` catch-all. It retries — **under a budget**,
`_EXIT_SEND_ATTEMPTS = 3`, on `entry.py`'s bounded-guard-retry precedent, so a broker
failing every send while answering every read cannot turn into an unbounded stream of
submissions — and the retry's whole licence is `DuplicateOrderGuard` re-asking the
broker at `guard_first_submission=True`, never this outcome's own answer. The
one-attempt rule is untouched for everything that still lands in `UNRESOLVED`.
→ [exit attempt taxonomy and halt scope](../decisions/2026-08-28-exit-attempt-taxonomy-and-halt-scope.md)

**A position whose exit attempt failed went dark, not just unmanaged.**
`check_once` skipped a position in `_attempted` whole — no `evaluate_triggers`, no
mark, nothing — conflating two separate concerns: "do not send another order"
(correct) and "do not price it any more" (never argued for). The spread that
triggered this release's incident went 76 minutes unpriced while the underlying
moved eight points back in its favour, and no Argus surface could say whether it was
still worth closing. **Fixed**: a spent position is now evaluated and recorded on
every tick, and only the SUBMISSION is suppressed. The mark it produces
(`cost_to_close`, `credit_stop`, `spot`, `still_triggered`) rides both the repeating
`watchdog.exit_spent_still_held` page and `/status`'s exit-attempts row, and `None`
renders as `unpriced` rather than as a number — a `0.0` on a row a human might act on
by hand would be the wrong kind of value, not a missing one. No new config key; this
half of the release is `src/` only.
→ [same doc](../decisions/2026-08-28-exit-attempt-taxonomy-and-halt-scope.md)

**A 0DTE position had no control that did not depend on a working market.**
`TriggerReason` had exactly two members, both price-based, and both had already
fired in the incident above before the position sat unclosed for over an hour — the
thing that costs money on a 0DTE product is not failing to *see* the loss, it is
seeing it, deciding, and being unable to *act*, and a second mechanism reading the
same broken market is not a second mechanism. **Fixed** with a third trigger,
`FLATTEN_BEFORE_CLOSE`: `watchdog.flatten_before_close_s` (1800.0) before **that
day's own close** — 15:30 ET on a normal session, **12:30 ET on a half-day**, since
the offset is applied to `MarketCalendar.close_time(d)` rather than to a literal, the
same rule and the same object `settlement_mark` already uses for exactly this reason.
It changes WHEN a position is closed, never HOW — same `ExitExecutor.close`, same
guarded walk, same width-capped market-crossing escalation — and it never invents a
price: an unpriceable flatten sends nothing and retries, because pricing it at the
spread's width would *guarantee* the maximum loss where holding to settlement
realizes at most it. It bypasses the sustain debounce deliberately (a clock reading
has no quote artifact to filter), and a position whose ordinary exit attempts are
already spent gets **one** further closing order past the mark — safe because the
send re-asks `DuplicateOrderGuard`, which permits only a broker-confirmed-dead order,
never because time has passed. One consequence the design did not anticipate: adding
a trigger with no market input made `TriggerEvaluation.blind` — the one evaluability
state that pages — unreachable, since it could never again be counted among the
"unknown" reasons; fixed by measuring `blind` against the market-dependent triggers
only.
→ [a clock is the third trigger](../decisions/2026-08-28-time-based-flatten-trigger.md)

**A sent-but-unfilled order was invisible to the concurrency cap.**
`RiskSnapshot.open_spread_equivalent` counted filled positions only —
`max(ceil(broker_legs / 2), ledger_spread_count)`, no term for an order still
working. Normally the price-walking loop owns that window for seconds; on
2026-08-28 an entry walk abandoned order 1424563 after a failed cancel and it sat
`working` for two hours, and had it filled it would have been a third spread
against a cap of two. **Fixed**: `open_spread_equivalent` gains a third term,
`working_open_spread_count`, which ADDS to the position-derived count (a pending
order is exposure distinct from a filled one). The value is the number of distinct
trade ideas with an OPENING order still working at the broker — polled by the
watchdog off `get_live_orders()` on its refresh cadence, read late-bound by
`RiskSnapshotBuilder`, exactly the wiring `ledger_spread_count` already uses.
Opening orders only: an exit's own order closes a position the cap already counts.
A failed poll never halts the watchdog — the count only gates new entries, and the
last good value stands (fail toward blocking). No new config key; `src/` only.
→ [count working orders toward the concurrency cap](../decisions/2026-08-29-count-working-orders-toward-concurrency-cap.md)

**A reconciliation halt parked the whole process, taking two healthy positions'
virtual stops with it.** `_halt_process`'s three effects — refuse new entries, end
the current session, prevent any future one — were welded to one `stop` event, so
a process-start order-level discrepancy (one unfilled working order, 30 points
OTM, no position implication) got the identical response a genuine position-level
mismatch deserves: the process parked, and because the watchdog only exists inside
a running session, both live 0DTE spreads went from one-watched-one-spent to
**neither watched**, three and three-quarter hours from settlement. The rail's
verdict was correct; its blast radius was the entire system. **Fixed**: a
reconciliation halt now carries a `HaltGrade`, derived by a pure classifier from
the discrepancy set alone — `ALL` (today's behaviour, and the default for every
non-reconciliation halt source, unchanged) or `ENTRIES`, earned only when there is
no position discrepancy of any kind, every order discrepancy is
`unexpected_working_order`, and every identifier parses to one of Argus's own
opening orders. Under `ENTRIES` the kill switch stays engaged and the halt stays
permanent — no auto-clear, still HALTED in red on `/status` — but the scheduler
runs and the day's session comes up with `submit=None`, so the watchdog runs and
held positions keep their virtual stops, their marks, and the 15:30 flatten. The
eligibility test deliberately compares no symbols or strikes (chained verticals
net a shared strike to zero in `positions`, so a symbol test would read "no
overlap" for exactly the strike two live spreads share), and the grade is
re-derived from a live broker answer every session, tightening only, never
relaxing. `_end_day`, the audit-write halt and the entry-path hard halt are
untouched — the classifier's input is a reconciliation verdict, and every other
halt source keeps `ALL` by default rather than by edit. No new config key; `src/`
only.
→ [a halt has one blast radius and needs two](../decisions/2026-08-29-halt-scope-split.md)

**On the one rail CLAUDE.md calls non-negotiable, "cancelled" was an intention
rather than an outcome.** The kill switch promises to halt new entries "including
an entry order already working at the exchange", and it keeps that promise through
`HaltCheckFn` → `_abort` → `_request_cancel` — which is best-effort and swallows
every exception. On 2026-08-28 order 1424563's DELETE came back a 502 at 10:10, the
walk finished anyway (correctly, by design), and **nothing in the process ever
asked about that order again**: it was still `working` at 12:15, when a restart's
reconciliation found it and halted. The same silence covers a cancel that was
ACCEPTED and stuck at `Cancel Requested`, which is why the fix triggers on the
STATE and never on the error — from outside, an order whose cancel raised and one
whose cancel is pending are the same object, a live order nobody is walking.
**Fixed** with a residual-order registry (`execution/residual_orders.py`):
`PriceWalkingLoop._finish` — the one funnel every walk ends through, and the
funnel every `_request_cancel` is immediately followed by — registers any order
whose last observation was non-terminal, and the watchdog steps the recheck on the
`LiveOrders` answer P4's working-order poll has already fetched (zero extra broker
requests, no new task). A **positively confirmed** WORKING sighting past
`watchdog.exit_cancel_settle_s` re-requests the cancel of an **OPENING** order on a
geometric backoff flooring at `watchdog.unmanaged_order_max_s` (900.0); a CLOSING
one is paged and never touched, because cancelling a close strands a position —
`HaltCheckFn`'s asymmetry, inherited verbatim. Absence is never a verdict in either
direction: it neither resolves a residual nor licenses a cancel. Direction comes
from what Argus **minted** (`state.sent.direction`, cross-checked against
`parse_external_identifier`) and never from the broker's echoed leg actions, which
are labelled relative to the account's position — the field is named
`minted_direction` and an AST test asserts the module reads no `.direction` at all.
Bounded on RATE, not COUNT, deliberately: a cancel is not a submission, and a
budget of three would have been spent inside the 10:00–10:13 outage and never
retried after cert recovered. The registry is **in-process and never rehydrated** —
a previous process's orphan is reconciliation's question, not this one's. **Ships
in `would_cancel` mode**: `watchdog.unmanaged_order_cancel` defaults to `false`, so
the same registry and the same decision are computed and recorded at Tier 2 with no
outbound call (`shadow.would_submit`'s shape, with the single call behind one flag
in one place, asserted structurally). Promoting is a config flip, not a redeploy of
code.
→ [a cancel that was requested is not a cancel that landed](../decisions/2026-08-29-mid-session-cancel-recheck.md)

## Deploy

**Deployed to the cert droplet 2026-08-30 (Sunday), active for the 2026-08-31
live-fire session.** The steps below are the record of what was run (and the
rollback path).

Rebuild AND config move together.

```bash
cd /opt/argus
scripts/argus-deploy.sh --dry-run v0.3.13   # show the plan, touch nothing
scripts/argus-deploy.sh v0.3.13             # deploy it
```

`argus.toml` gains `watchdog.flatten_before_close_s` (required, 1800.0) and
`watchdog.unmanaged_order_max_s` (required, 900.0), plus
`watchdog.unmanaged_order_cancel = false` (optional, defaults to `false`); `git
checkout v0.3.13` brings all three in the same commit as the code that needs them.

**P5 deploys in observer mode.** `unmanaged_order_cancel = false` means the recheck
registers, classifies and records every residual and sends no cancel. Promote by
setting it to `true` in `argus.toml` and restarting — no rebuild. What to look for
first: `order.residual_registered` (they should appear after abandoned walks and
mostly resolve on the next poll), then `order.residual_would_cancel` (rare by
construction — most residuals resolve inside the settle allowance, so do NOT wait
for one before promoting) and any `order.residual_unmanaged` page.

**Rollback needs BOTH halves.** `git checkout v0.3.12` (or whichever prior tag)
reverts the config too — confirm with `git status --short` after checkout, same
discipline every rebuild-plus-config release since v0.3.7 has called out.

## Scope

**Closes ALL SEVEN of P0–P6 from the 2026-08-28 exit-attempt-taxonomy record —
finding 8 (P5) included.** All seven are confirmed with Richard as v0.3.13's
scope, decided 2026-08-30, no further waiting. An earlier draft of this document
held P3 (and, by inheritance, P5) out for a separate deploy — that read a design
sign-off as also covering a release-scope call it never settled; corrected
2026-08-29. A later draft still hedged P5 as conditional on beating the tag —
that hedge is also gone: P5 lands here regardless of build/tag ordering.

P0/P1 built the same evening the incident was found; P2 is the finding the record
called out as having *no mitigation at all*. P0/P1/P2 land together rather than
split, on the same reasoning v0.3.12 gave for its own three fixes: nothing there
is independently useful before the others, and P2's send allowance depends on
P0's `NOTHING_AT_BROKER` / `DuplicateOrderGuard` retry precedent.

**P3** (finding 7) is described in full above, under "Fixed" —
[a halt has one blast radius and needs two](../decisions/2026-08-29-halt-scope-split.md).
Designed first and signed off before any code, per the weekend
[P3–P6 scope authorization](../tasks/argus_p3_p4_p5_p6_v0313_scope_prompt.md)'s
explicit gate. `src/` only, no new config key — the config move is P2's and P5's.

**P4** (finding 6, also described above under "Fixed"): working opening orders
now count toward `max_concurrent_positions`. Built 2026-08-29 under the same
weekend authorization — mechanical, additive, `src/` only, no new config key, and
independent of P0–P3.

**P6**: `WalkResult` and `WalkResult.to_payload()` gain a `reject_reason` field,
populated from the rejected `Order`'s broker prose on a `REJECTED` outcome and
`None` (never `""`) otherwise. v0.3.12 already put the broker's reason on the
`order.event` record; P6 puts it on the SAME surface the entry/exit escalation
paths and alerts already read `WalkResult.reason` from (`entry_rejected`,
`entry.finished`, `ExitReport`), so an operator no longer has to correlate a
walk's outcome against a separate `order.event` row by hand. Second half:
`entry._check_for_duplicate` now catches the messageless `TimeoutError` that
`asyncio.timeout` raises and re-labels it (same type — `error_type` readers are
unaffected) with what it waited for and for how long. Built 2026-08-29 under the
same weekend authorization as P4; `src/` only, no new config key, no deploy
coupling.

**P5** (finding 8) is BUILT and described in full above, under "Fixed" —
[a cancel that was requested is not a cancel that landed](../decisions/2026-08-29-mid-session-cancel-recheck.md).
Designed first and signed off before any code, per the weekend authorization's
explicit gate; built 2026-08-30 after that review. It is the only item in this
release that adds a new automated outbound broker action, and it ships in
`would_cancel` mode for exactly that reason — the decision runs and records, the
call does not, and promoting it is a config flip rather than a deploy. Adds the
REQUIRED `watchdog.unmanaged_order_max_s`, riding the config cutover P2 already
forces.

**Closes finding 8.** A failed — or merely un-landed — cancel now has a
mid-session follow-up: the order is watched until the broker is seen to say it is
terminal, and an opening one gets its cancel re-requested on a bounded rate rather
than waiting for the next restart's reconciliation to find it. Finding 7 (P3) is
closed by this release as well. **What it does not fix, deliberately:** an order
left by a PREVIOUS process. The registry is in-memory, so 2026-08-28's order would
still have halted the 12:15 restart — graded `ENTRIES` now, so with a watchdog and
both spreads still watched. That recovery is still cancel-by-hand-and-restart, and
it is reconciliation's question rather than this rail's.

**Three things worth carrying forward, not decisions but consequences worth watching**:

- The exit path's one-attempt rule now bends in exactly two, narrowly-scoped,
  broker-reconfirmed ways (`NOTHING_AT_BROKER`'s budgeted retry, the flatten's
  one-shot override past the mark) rather than the uniform retry the record's open
  decision explicitly warned against building. The taxonomy — which outcomes may
  retry and on what licence — is the thing that makes both safe; neither should be
  generalized without re-deriving it.
- P2 cannot be validated any better in cert than anything else on this path is: the
  scenario that exercises the flatten's send allowance needs a position still held
  and spent at the mark, which sandbox has never produced and cannot be forced.
- P5's `would_cancel` stage validates its INPUTS thoroughly and its TRIGGER almost
  never, and that asymmetry is expected rather than a shortfall: most residuals
  resolve on the first poll, so the condition that fires a cancel is rare by
  construction. Size the stage accordingly — a week, not a month — and do not treat
  the absence of an `order.residual_would_cancel` record as a reason to keep
  waiting. P5 should also be on whatever reviews `execution.walk_source` /
  `watchdog.trigger_source` before size is scaled: by then it should either have a
  cert track record or be named as a gap, not have ridden in and never been looked
  at again.

**The daily max-loss circuit breaker remains structurally inert in sandbox**,
unchanged by this release. Proving that rail out is still a live-flip gate.
