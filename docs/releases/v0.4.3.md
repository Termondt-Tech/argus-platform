## What changed

**1. The width cap (`src/`).** A vertical cannot be worth more than the distance
between its strikes — the long leg caps it, which is what "defined risk" means — so a
price above the width is arithmetically impossible for the instrument. `_cross_limit`
has always held that (`min(debit, market.width)`) and was **the only place in the system
that did**. Two clamps close the gap:

- `build._limit_price` takes a REQUIRED keyword-only `width` (from `SpreadSignal.width`)
  and clamps through a new `_cap_at_width`. This is where the production breach was.
- `PriceWalk.from_policy` takes an optional `width` and clamps the floor **last**, after
  the percentage, the minimum and the credit clamp. `Decimal.copy_sign` keeps it
  direction-free, so the state machine keeps having no direction branch.
- `Order.width` is new: the strike distance read off the legs' own OCC symbols, **not a
  quote**, which is what lets the walk cap itself while importing no market data — a
  rung must stay computable in a dead book.

It is a **cap, not a refusal**: there is a real price, it is merely above a ceiling the
instrument defines, so the honest answer is the ceiling. Refusing would strand a
deep-ITM 0DTE spread with no automated path to close.

**2. The offer is now recorded beside the mark (`src/`, telemetry only).**
`cost_to_close` is the floored MID; `crossing_debit` is the short ask minus the long bid
— what a cross actually fills at. The gap between them is the whole question this pass
could not answer, and it existed in the record only where a cross had already fired
(n=3 across 780,853 rows, right-censored by `min(debit, width)`). Now:

- **every mark** — `TriggerEvaluation.crossing_debit` rides `watchdog.check`,
  `watchdog.degraded`, `watchdog.check_spent` and the unpriceable pages, so at the 1s
  cadence the offer distance is recorded continuously for every held position;
- **`watchdog.exit_started`** — RAW, uncapped, alongside `order.limit_price` (where the
  walk opens) and `walk_away_price` (where it stops), so one record answers *was the
  offer inside the ladder's range?*

**Nothing decides on it** — the standing `scan_age_s` has. `None`, never NaN, when the
legs cannot be priced.

## Why 3, and why `exit_walk_away_pct` did not move

The full argument is in the decision record. In brief:

- **The band is empty and the gap is arithmetic.** 12 flatten exits opened at
  0.05/0.10, 23 credit-stop exits at 0.95–1.95, nothing between. A credit stop opens at
  `credit_multiple × entry_credit` = 2.0 × [0.50…1.00] by construction; a flatten opens
  at whatever a near-worthless 0DTE winner is worth. So a floor of 3 or fewer steps
  binds only below |0.75| and touches the flatten rows alone — **zero of the 23
  credit-stop rows is under 0.95, measured rather than assumed.** That is what retires
  2026-08-31's claim that 1 is the unique safe value: true at n=2, false at n=37, its
  reasoning intact and its conclusion sample-limited.
- **An unreached rung is free.** The walk stops at the fill, a limit walking the grid
  cannot skip a resting offer, and the cross that follows is already capped at the
  width — so a wider floor can only fill at or below what the cross would have paid.
  Narrowing therefore buys nothing, and 3 costs $10 per rung *reached* against up to
  the full width (~$900 at 2 contracts) for an exhausted ladder, in the one regime
  where the cross can be absent entirely. 2026-08-31 proved it can:
  `CROSS_UNPRICEABLE`, attempt spent in 1.3 seconds, held to expiry.
- **`min_steps = 1` is proven, which is why raising it is a small step and not a
  gamble.** 4/4 flatten walks in the near-worthless band walked away at 0 steps before
  it reached cert; 0 of 5 after, and one actually used its rung (2026-09-09, step 1,
  −0.15).
- **`exit_walk_away_pct` governs only the credit-stop regime and nothing there is
  broken.** Every credit-stop exit that filled did so at step 0, 1 or once 4, inside
  the 4–8 rungs it provisions. Lowering it to 0.10 would halve that range on cert FILL
  evidence whose bias runs the wrong way: if cert fills too easily it *overstates* how
  little concession is needed, so 17/18 exit fills landing within one rung can never
  argue for fewer rungs. Widening it is the principled direction, and that is the
  functional-form change below rather than a value tweak.

## The width cap fired — which is why it is fixed here

The sharpest thing the pass found, and the reason this release grew a `src/` half:

```
2026-09-03 15:37:59 ET   argus-close-20260903-call-7725-7730
  width 5.00   entry_credit 0.70   2 contracts
  opening limit   -5.05      <- already past the width
  walk-away floor -6.05      <- 20 rungs, the last 21 unpayable by construction
  filled at step 0 at -5.05  ->  realized -870.00
  holding to settlement pays the width: (0.70 - 5.00) x 2 x 100 = -860.00
```

The flatten paid $870 to avoid an $860 loss. CLAUDE.md recorded that position at
−860.00, "identical to holding it to settlement"; the ledger says −870.00, and the $10
is exactly the 0.05 by which the limit exceeded the most the spread could ever be
worth. **The breach was at the opening limit, not the floor** — `_limit_price` refuses
only a non-positive price, and `cost_to_close` came off a wide print on a deep-ITM
spread.

**Recomputed with the cap in place: the exit prices at −5.00 and realizes −860.00 —
exactly equal to holding to settlement.** The $10 was the whole of the excess.

Had it not filled on the first rung, the −6.05 floor would have kept offering more:
walking it to the end is `(0.70 − 6.05) × 2 × 100 = −1,070` against an −860 maximum —
**$210 worse than doing nothing**, and past `max_loss_per_position = 950` too, on the
one rail whose entire premise is that loss is bounded by the width. So the exposure was
never $10; it was up to ~$210 per spread at current size.

What the floor cap changes, measured:

| exit opening limit | floor before | floor after | rungs after |
|---|---|---|---|
| −5.00 | −6.00 | **−5.00** | 0 |
| −4.50 | −5.40 | **−5.00** | 10 |
| −4.00 | −4.80 | −4.80 (inside the width) | 16 |
| −1.65 | −2.00 | −2.00 | 7 |
| −0.05 | −0.20 | −0.20 | 3 |

The −4.50 row is the case the 2026-08-31 record named and left. The change **narrows**
the walk in the deep regime — the safe direction — and touches `exit_walk_away_pct`'s
value not at all. **The entry ladder is bit-identical**, by arithmetic rather than by a
flag: an opening walk concedes downward from a credit already capped at the width, so
`abs(floor) ≤ abs(initial) ≤ width` holds before the clamp is reached.

## Deploying it

Read the `--dry-run` plan first — on a coupled release it shows both halves, and the
config diff is the only thing that will ever describe the config change:

```
/opt/argus/scripts/argus-deploy.sh --dry-run v0.4.3
```

**Not under `sudo`** — the script refuses root and exits 2, because running it that way
would make its `git` calls run as root (the documented `sudo git` incident). It invokes
`sudo` itself for docker, systemctl, journalctl and reading `host.env`. *(Corrected
2026-09-18; these notes originally prefixed the command with `sudo`.)*

Expect the plan to report `build: YES` / `config: YES` / `restart: YES`. After the real
run, **both** read-backs must pass: `version: v0.4.3` and `config.sha fcdf969302dd`
(profile `live`, filename `argus.live.toml`) on the startup line and on `/status`. The
deploy script asserts both and fails if either is stale. A `version` still reading
`v0.4.2` here **is** a failed deploy on this class — it means the restart reattached to
the previous image.

Timing: this restarts the trading process, so it belongs after the cash close and 0DTE
settlement, with no session in progress and nothing held. A restart with a live
position open is the one thing this class must not do casually — the watchdog comes back
and re-adopts, but there is no reason to exercise that here.

## What this release does NOT do

- **`credit_multiple` is untouched.** The prohibition stands: conclusions only from
  `entry_credit_basis = realized`, and a number measured in cert cannot size it alone.
- **`underlying_buffer_points`, `sustained_s`, `flatten_before_close_s` untouched.**
  One exit fired on the underlying backstop (2026-09-01 18:42) — that is the rail
  working, not a calibration signal. The 1800s flatten runway is what the data
  *supports*: it is precisely what makes three rungs free.
- **The entry-walk half of the 2026-08-10 debt is still owed.** `execution.walk_source`
  still carries sandbox-entry values, and `entry_drift_abort_points`' admissible
  computation is still not run.
- **The walk-away INSTANT is still not recorded.** `_cross_limit` collapses three
  distinct causes into one `None` and discards the debit it saw, so `CROSS_REFUSED` and
  `CROSS_UNPRICEABLE` carry no market state. And `check_once` skips any key in
  `_exiting`, so the mark goes dark for the 4–23s a walk runs — `exit_started` and
  `watchdog.crossed` bracket it without covering the middle. Both need design, not a
  one-liner: the first changes a return shape and adds an `ExitReport` field (which has
  converters downstream — the 2026-09-02 under-copy hazard).
- **Replacing the percentage** with an absolute tick/time budget capped at width, which
  **deletes** `exit_walk_min_steps` rather than tuning it, since that key exists only to
  patch the percentage's low end. It is why the two keys have always been one decision:
  they are one parameter.
- **It does not fix the zero-mid refusal**, and that is deliberate. When a spread is
  nearly worthless both legs quote `0.00/0.05`, the net mid is 0.00, and
  `_limit_price` cannot express a limit of 0.00 — so the flatten builds no order at
  all, and cannot fall through to the cross either (the cross is only reached after a
  walk-away, and there is no walk to abandon). This happened live on 2026-09-17 for
  ~63 seconds before a quote moved and it filled at 0.05. It is the **opposite end** of
  the same missing bound the width cap just closed — no ceiling there, no floor here —
  but its severity is low and its fix is a policy call, not a bug fix: a net mid of 0.00
  means the spread is nearly worthless, so the position that cannot be closed is also
  the one with almost no risk. `exit_walk_min_steps` cannot reach it; the refusal is
  upstream of the floor.
