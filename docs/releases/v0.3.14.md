# v0.3.14 — Grumium

*TAGGED, RELEASED, AND DEPLOYED 2026-09-01 ~17:02 EDT. Prepared 2026-09-01 from what is already on `main` (commits
`3be57b5`..`3af80bf`). Three fixes, all from v0.3.13's two cert live-fire days
(2026-08-31 and 2026-09-01), and all the same shape underneath: **a watch that was
supposed to survive a disruption did not.** The exit ladder did not survive a dead
feed — the one regime where it is the more robust half of the escalation, it had no
range at all. The kill switch did not survive a restart — `_engaged` was `False`
unconditionally on every boot, so a `/kill` an operator pressed stopped being in
force the moment the droplet rebooted, silently. And a soft entry stand-down did
not leave the watchdog running — `_end_day` set the session `stop` event, which
also stops the virtual stops, the marks and the 15:30 flatten for the rest of
expiry day.*

*Named per the Argus's Eyes convention (`docs/releases/README.md`). **Grumium** —
ξ Draconis — qualifies by position, not etymology (the word is "the jaw"): it is
one of the four stars of Draco's head, the cluster that already gave **Eltanin**
(γ Dra) to v0.3.13. Draco is the dragon Ladon, the guardian that never slept, and
from northern latitudes Draco is circumpolar — it never sets, never drops below the
horizon, never "restarts." That is this release exactly: the halt and the watchdog
now stay up across the thing that used to take them down. **Rastaban stays reserved**
— its dual-watchdog / consensus reading is a different shape and nothing here adds a
second independent verification loop.*

**Requires an image rebuild AND a config update, and they must deploy together.**
`config.py`, `execution/loop.py`, `execution/entry.py`, `execution/watchdog.py`,
`risk/killswitch.py`, `risk/kill_state.py` (new), `control.py`, `status.py`,
`app.py`, `session.py` all change. `argus.toml`/`argus.dev.toml` gain **one** new
**required** key — `watchdog.exit_walk_min_steps` (1). An old config against the new
image refuses to start (`missing required config key watchdog.exit_walk_min_steps`);
the new key means nothing to the old image. Same coupling `watchdog.flatten_before_close_s`
(v0.3.13), `[reconcile]` (v0.3.11) and `staleness.greeks_max_age_s` (v0.3.12) each
already forced. **No migration** — the kill-switch persistence work reads the audit
log through the existing schema and adds three new Tier 1 categories
(`kill_switch.resume`, `kill_switch.restored`, `kill_switch.restore_failed`), no
column, no table.

## Fixed

**The exit price ladder had no range in the regime the flatten fires in, so P2's
first-ever fire rode entirely on a single market-crossing limit.** On 2026-08-31 —
v0.3.13's first live-fire day and `FLATTEN_BEFORE_CLOSE`'s first fire — both 0DTE
credit spreads triggered the flatten at 15:30:00, correctly and to the second, and
then the ladder contributed **nothing**: both walked away `after 0 step(s) at
-0.05`. One spread's crossing limit found an offer and filled; the other's could
not be priced (`no offer on the short leg?`), spent the position's one exit attempt
in 1.3 seconds, and held to expiry. `PriceWalk.from_policy` computed the walk-away
floor as `round_to_tick(initial − |initial| × walk_away_pct)`, and a percentage of a
price vanishes below half a tick: at `exit_walk_away_pct = 0.20` that is any exit
opening at a debit of **0.10 or less**, which for a winning 0DTE spread being
flattened at 15:30 is not a tail — it is the modal case, because a winning spread is
nearly worthless by then. The credit direction has a degenerate-case guard right
there in the function; the debit direction never had one. **Fixed** with
`WalkPolicy.min_concession_steps`, a floor under the percentage expressed in
**reprices** (so it survives `exit_step_ticks` being raised), applied direction-free
in `from_policy` — the asymmetry lives on the policy, whose contract already says
entry and exit differ only there, and `for_entry` passes `0` explicitly. Shipped at
`watchdog.exit_walk_min_steps = 1`: the **unique** value that changes the two broken
rows (−0.05 and −0.10 now get one rung) and no row that already works (−0.15 through
−0.75 are bit-identical at 1). The two guards compose in the safe order — the
minimum first, the existing credit clamp after — so a credit walk given a nonzero
minimum still cannot be pushed across zero. Cost at current size is at most **$10
per spread**, only for exits opening at 0.05 or 0.10, and **the expected sign is
negative** — the alternative to the rung is the cross that immediately follows,
priced at the short leg's ask minus the long leg's bid, and a limit walking up the
grid cannot skip a resting offer. Nothing touches `_cross_limit`'s width cap.
→ [the exit ladder had no range in the regime the flatten fires in](../decisions/2026-08-31-exit-ladder-near-tick.md),
[the flatten's first fire](../decisions/2026-08-31-flatten-first-fire-pass.md)

**`CROSS_UNPRICEABLE` no longer spends the exit attempt.** The second half of the
same day's failure: the put's cross could not be priced — a 45-point-OTM 0DTE short
leg at 15:40 had no offer to lift — and `CROSS_UNPRICEABLE` landed in the
`is not WALKED_AWAY` catch-all, consuming both the ordinary attempt and the
flatten's one-shot override on a transient feed state. **Fixed**: it is now
retryable, in exactly the shape P0 (v0.3.13) gave `NOTHING_AT_BROKER` — rebuilt on
the next cadence tick and submitted **through `DuplicateOrderGuard`**, sharing P0's
single `_EXIT_SEND_ATTEMPTS` budget via `_RETRYABLE_EXITS` (a mapping, so the
one-attempt rule's exception list stays one greppable object). The case is stronger
than P0's, not weaker: `CROSS_UNPRICEABLE` is unreachable except **through**
`_await_settled` returning `outcome=None`, the single most demanding confirmation in
the module — `_cross` already acts on that same confirmation by submitting a second
closing order *unguarded*, and the retry responds to it *guarded*. **`CROSS_REFUSED`
is untouched**, is not in the mapping, and returns before `_cross_limit` is ever
called. One alert moved: the watchdog's escalation now raises
`watchdog_cross_unpriceable` on its own tag (`_CROSS_UNPRICEABLE`, separate from
`_NOTHING_SENT` — on a phone they read alike but the operator's next move is
opposite, broker versus feed), with `candidate_id` at the top level so B1's throttle
scopes it by trade idea.
→ [the exit ladder](../decisions/2026-08-31-exit-ladder-near-tick.md)

**The kill switch did not survive a restart — the rail CLAUDE.md calls a
non-negotiable was state of a process, not state of the world.**
`KillSwitch.__init__` set `self._engaged = False` unconditionally and the object
held no persistence. A trip writes a durable Tier 1 `kill_switch.trip` row and
**nothing ever read it back**, so:

```
10:00  operator presses POST /kill      → _engaged = True, kill_switch.trip written
10:05  OOM / deploy / droplet reboot    → restart (unless-stopped, Restart=always)
10:05  new process: _engaged = False    → reconciles clean, session starts
10:06  entries resume
```

Nothing fired — `engage` was never called in the new process, and the alert lives
inside `engage` — and `/status` misreported it *in the reassuring direction*, because
`status.py` gated the trip row it had just read behind `if ks.engaged`: the page
rendered *armed (not engaged)* with a null source and a null `since` while the row
sat right there in the DB. Fourth instance of a pattern the decisions index already
names three times. **In cert a spurious resume re-proposes cert orders; live, the
halt an operator pressed silently stops being in force, and the restart is invisible
to the person who pressed it — a live-flip blocker.** **Fixed**: the switch is now
built from the audit log — engaged **iff the latest `kill_switch.trip` has no
`kill_switch.resume` at a higher `seq`** (`risk/kill_state.py`; two O(1)
index-backed queries, `seq` never `ts`, and **no date bound** — the deliberate
opposite of the breaker's day-scoped read, which is why it is its own file). It
never expires and never auto-clears. `POST /resume?confirm=yes` is the **only** path
to `KillSwitch.disengage` anywhere (an AST test pins it) — human, bearer-authed,
audited, Tailscale-only, and deliberately not one-tap because it is the only route
in the system that *enables* risk. `disengage` records **before** it flips (the
inverse of `engage`, because the ordering follows the safety direction: a failed
clear must be a no-op). Rehydration **restores the switch and never `HaltRecord`** —
it reproduces the state the trip left, not a stronger one, and the switch's own
three-point entry scope is already exactly monitor-only, so no `HaltGrade` is
involved. An unreadable state fails closed to ENGAGED; a restored start pages once
(`kill_switch.restored`) without writing a second trip, so `/status` still shows the
original press time, now as `ENGAGED (RESTORED)`. `/resume` is idempotent
(`not_engaged`, no row) exactly as `/kill` is (`already_tripped`, no row).
→ [the kill switch does not survive a restart](../decisions/2026-09-01-kill-switch-persistence.md)

**A soft entry stand-down took the watchdog, the marks and the 15:30 flatten with
it.** `EntryExecutor._end_day` ended today's entries by setting `self._stop` — the
**session** stop event, handed to four consumers: the watchdog's scan loop, the exit
walk, the `run_session` journal loop (whose `finally` tears everything down), and
the scheduler (which then sleeps to the next trading day). So a transient cert blip
that spent `execution.guard_unreachable_max_streak` bursts back to back — the
`_guard_unreachable` path, which has cost trading days (2026-08-25, 10:00:00 ET, the
day's first tick) — silently did three things in ascending severity: a held 0DTE
spread stopped being watched (no virtual stop, no mark, and since P2 no time-based
flatten — the day's designed exit); `/status` went quiet about it, rendering a still-held
position as a session that ended normally; and **an exit already walking was
cancelled AND spent** (`ABORTED` → `NOT_RESOLVED`, not in `_RETRYABLE_EXITS`), the
sharpest harm and the one the finding did not name. **Fixed**: `_end_day` is renamed
`_end_entries_today` and sets a session-scoped `EntriesEnded` — a **frozen
dataclass**, not a second event, with one reader (`submit`, refusing right after the
kill-switch check) and no `clear()`; it narrows the ACTION rather than adding the
second stop event P3 explicitly rejected. `SignalLoop` is not modified at all — the
loop keeps spinning and producing the calibration record, which is the whole point
of the day. Same runtime posture as `HaltGrade.ENTRIES`, opposite in every other
respect: no kill switch, no `HaltRecord`, clears at the rollover with no human step —
so `/status` renders them as two rows (`EntriesEndedToday`), never folded into one
`monitor_only` flag. The other `_end_day` site (`entry_build_failed`, a code defect,
nothing in its `try` touches the network) shares the watchdog fix and gets none of
the retry treatment. The entry stand-down itself is **untouched** — "never soften
the ENTRY stand-down" still holds; this softens only what else the stand-down
reached.
→ [`_end_day` ends the day, and the day includes the watchdog](../decisions/2026-09-01-end-day-keeps-the-watchdog.md)

## Deploy

Rebuild AND config move together.

```bash
cd /opt/argus
scripts/argus-deploy.sh --dry-run v0.3.14   # show the plan, touch nothing
scripts/argus-deploy.sh v0.3.14             # deploy it
```

`argus.toml` gains `watchdog.exit_walk_min_steps` (required, `1`); `git checkout
v0.3.14` brings it in the same commit as the code that needs it. Nothing else in the
config moves — the audit-rate comment block around `[audit] db_path` was updated with
the 2026-08-31 numbers but carries no key change.

### ONE-TIME: the first boot restores every un-resumed trip in history

**Check for a standing trip BEFORE you restart, not after.** The persistence read has
no date bound — deliberately, because a Friday `/kill` must still be in force on
Monday — so the first process to run this image evaluates *the entire history* of
`kill_switch.trip` against a `kill_switch.resume` category that **did not exist until
this release**. Any trip ever written and never manually reconciled comes back
ENGAGED on that first boot, however old it is.

This is not hypothetical: **it fired on the 2026-09-01 cutover.** The boot at 17:02:24
EDT restored `trip_seq 496115` — the 2026-08-28 12:15 ET `reconciliation_mismatch`
halt, the one whose 1 discrepancy was abandoned order 1424563 — and the four sessions
since had each come up `_engaged = False` and traded straight through it (2026-08-31,
2 positions; 2026-09-01, 8 positions). The restore behaved exactly as designed —
`kill_switch.restored` at Tier 1, one page, the ORIGINAL press time preserved on
`/status` as `ENGAGED (RESTORED)`, `halted.halted: false` because rehydration restores
the switch and never `HaltRecord` — and the same boot's `reconcile.result` came back
`ok: true` with both discrepancy lists empty, confirming the originating condition was
long gone. **The rail was right and the deploy was surprised by it**, which is a
documentation failure rather than a defect.

Before the cutover restart:

```bash
sqlite3 "file:argus.db?mode=ro" "SELECT seq, ts, payload FROM audit_log
  WHERE category = 'kill_switch.trip' ORDER BY seq DESC LIMIT 1;"
```

If a row comes back, that halt WILL be in force after the restart. Assess it, then
clear it with the route — never by editing the DB:

```bash
curl -sS -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  "http://<host>:8443/resume?confirm=yes&reason=<why>" | jq .
```

`restart_required` in the response is a report, not a condition: it is `true` only if
the live process is also holding a `HaltRecord`, which a *restored* trip never
creates. On the 2026-09-01 cutover it was `false` — the clear alone was enough, no
second restart. From the second deploy onward this section is dead letter; the backlog
exists exactly once.

**Rollback needs BOTH halves.** `git checkout v0.3.13` (or whichever prior tag)
reverts the config too — confirm with `git status --short` after checkout, the same
discipline every rebuild-plus-config release since v0.3.7 has called out. There is no
migration to reverse; the three new audit categories are additive and a prior image
simply never writes them.

**After deploy, what to look for:**

- `watchdog.check` ticks continuing through any `entry.entries_ended_today` /
  `entry.aborted_day` event — a held spread should keep its mark and, at
  `watchdog.flatten_before_close_s` before close, still fire `FLATTEN_BEFORE_CLOSE`.
- On any restart while a `/kill` is in force: `kill_switch.restored` at start,
  `/status` reading `ENGAGED (RESTORED)` with the **original** press time, and no new
  `kill_switch.trip`. A clean restart writes nothing new. **Confirmed on the
  2026-09-01 cutover** — all four properties observed, on a four-day-old trip.
- `kill_switch.engaged: false` on `/status` before the next session opens, if the
  cutover restored a stale trip and you cleared it. A restored trip that is left
  standing costs the next session every entry — the watchdog, the marks and the
  flatten still run, so it is a no-trade day rather than a blind one, but it is
  silent from the entry side and easy to miss until the day is over.
- An exit opening at a 0.05 or 0.10 debit now logging `walked_away after 1 step(s)`
  rather than `0 step(s)`.

## Scope

**Closes findings 1 and 2 from the 2026-08-31 flatten-first-fire pass** (the exit
ladder collapse and `CROSS_UNPRICEABLE` spending the attempt — both designed first,
signed off, and built the same day, the gate P3/P5 took, because both touch exit
price construction and the one-attempt rule), **the kill-switch persistence
live-flip blocker** raised 2026-09-01, and **the `_end_day`-takes-the-watchdog
finding** (a residual of v0.3.13's P3, which corrected the halt-side version of the
same defect but did not touch `_end_day`).

**Deliberately NOT in this release**, all reported and left standing:

- The **entry** walk's identical near-tick collapse below a 0.25 credit, unreachable
  today only because `signal.min_credit = 0.50` and by nothing that says so.
  `for_entry` passes `min_concession_steps = 0` explicitly to make that provable.
- `execution.walk_source` / `watchdog.trigger_source` still carry SANDBOX-ENTRY
  values, and the owed data-backed pass now also owes `exit_walk_min_steps` and
  `exit_walk_away_pct` a re-derivation from real `walk.finished` outcome mix.
  Raising `exit_walk_min_steps` past 1 is a **config flip, not a deploy**.
- The **walk's floor has no width cap** where `_cross_limit` does — a −4.50 exit
  floors at −5.40 on a 5-wide spread. Unreachable at `exit_walk_min_steps = 1` (max
  floor magnitude 0.30) but real if the key is raised.
- `staleness.market_data_max_age_s = 2.0` governing the **whole** exit path, not just
  entry pricing — the 571s the 2026-08-31 put spent unpriceable came from that
  entry-sized bound. **Must not be fixed by raising that key** (it also governs the
  entry gate's staleness rail).
- **`FillInfo.fill_price` captured durably** — 2026-09-01 confirmed at n=8, with a
  realized consequence, that `OpenSpread.entry_credit` (the walk's LIMIT) runs low,
  making every `credit_multiple` stop marginally early. Still filed as deferred
  (column + migration); this release does not touch it.
- The **`credit_stop` bare-`>=` float-noise** stop on `watchdog.py:577`
  (`call-7670-7675` stopped on 4.4e-16 on 2026-09-01) — the 2026-08-04 mark-tool
  defect on the live trigger path. Not in this release.
- **`_flatten_attempted` claimed before `build_order` can fail** — the flatten's
  one-shot past-the-mark override is spent once the legs have quotes, but
  `_limit_price` can still refuse a net mid rounding to 0.00, the flatten's modal
  regime. Reported 2026-09-01, not fixed here.
- **`_reprice` never inspects `replaced.status`** — a walk whose original order fills
  mid-replace records the rejected replacement as `NOT_RESOLVED` / `rejected` for a
  trade idea that actually filled (`put-7615-7610`, 2026-09-01: a succeeded exit
  recorded as a failure). The FILLED check is identifier-wide in `_await_settled` but
  not at `_resolve`'s terminal branch. Consequential — the entry mirror shares
  `_reprice` and must not feed the consecutive-rejection back-off decision — and
  **not in this release.**

**The daily max-loss circuit breaker remains structurally inert in sandbox**,
unchanged by this release. Proving that rail out is still a live-flip gate.
