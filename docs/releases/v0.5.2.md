## Name

**Mira** — ο Ceti, Latin for "the wonderful" (Hevelius, 1662), the first variable star
ever recognised: it fades below naked-eye visibility for months and returns on a rhythm.

**Found outside the pool, which is empty** (Rastaban, its last reservation, went to
v0.5.1). It qualifies loosely — by tradition rather than etymology, the standing given
to Polaris and Alterf — and it is fair to say it is not "an eye". The fit is the
release's shape: a spread the market has faded to nothing, and a watchman that must
decide whether a thing that has gone quiet is *gone* or merely *dim*. The whole release
turns on that distinction — a quiet quote read as a dead one (finding 3, closed by
measurement), and a nearly worthless position that is deliberately left to fade rather
than paid to be looked at. Richard kept it (2026-09-26).

## What changed

### A. The config moves, and package B

#### A1. The profit target stays OFF

`watchdog.profit_target_enabled = false` in all three profiles. **This release was prepared
to switch it on**; package B keeps it off
([decision](../decisions/2026-09-26-strike-touch-stop.md#why-the-profit-target-is-off)).

- **What was established, and still holds:** a one-tick take beats holding to the flatten
  *for one position* (2026-09-22). v0.5.1 discharged the stub-close gate on promoting it.
- **What the per-position analysis could not see:** at two slots a take frees a slot, and
  the slot is refilled.
- **At the book level it makes days more volatile, consistently** (day SD +25–140% in the
  replay). Its mean effect flips sign between marks, between the 950 and 1,050 caps, and
  between refilling and not refilling
  ([exploration §3](../../RESULTS.md)).
- **Off keeps the first live week of the new stop and the side rule attributable.** On
  later is a config-only release, preferably once the side rule is proven, since it
  contains the take's variance.
- The trigger is still EVALUATED and recorded on every tick (`profit_target` in
  `watchdog.check`), so its would-fire record keeps accumulating.
- **At one contract the stub cannot arise**, so the stub close and the quantity-aware guard
  sit dormant. They stay armed (`stub_close_enabled = true`).

#### A2. The caps: 2 spreads × 1 contract at 475, from 1 × 2 at 950

| Key | v0.5.1 | v0.5.2 |
|---|---|---|
| `risk.max_concurrent_positions` | 1 | **2** |
| `risk.max_loss_per_position` | 950.0 | **475.0** |
| `risk.max_contracts_per_position` | 2 | **1** |
| `risk.daily_max_loss` | 950.0 | **1050.0** (live and sandbox; dev keeps its declared 1740) |

**Open risk does not scale; the day's loss cap rises by 100.**

- **The welded invariant holds, no longer with equality.** `max_concurrent_positions ×
  max_loss_per_position ≤ daily_max_loss` is `2 × 475 = 950 ≤ 1050`; it was `1 × 950 =
  950`. `tests/test_config_profiles.py` pins it for every profile.
- **What is open at once is unchanged** (950), the phase is still live at minimum size,
  and `position_size_pct` is untouched.
- **What rises is the DAY.** The loss budget (2026-09-21) and the daily breaker both
  bound it at `daily_max_loss`, now 1050. The budget still counts every open vertical at
  its full width.
- **Why 1050: the second slot survives one ordinary stop.**
  - A 5-wide lot at the 0.50 floor has a worst case of about 450, so the slack above two
    open lots is `daily_max_loss − 2 × ~450`.
  - At 950 that is ~50, less than one ordinary stop (~60–90 a lot). The budget then
    refuses the second lot while the first is open, and the replay had both slots filled
    only ~50–65% of the time any position was open.
  - At 1050 the slack is ~150, and the share is ~72–86%.
  - Expected P&L did not measurably differ
    ([exploration note §2](../../RESULTS.md)).
- **G1 (the breaker proven on a real losing day) is still open,** now at 1050.

The evidence is [the two-slot research](../../RESULTS.md),
which is **not a decision record** (nothing in `docs/research/` is, until a decision cites
it). This release ships its §5.2 restructure **with its §5.1 rule** (§A5); what it measured
and did not:

- **What it measured.** Across 23 sessions and four path models, the as-built two-slot
  book at one lot each holds the opposite side on about 70% of days, by accident (the
  second entry arrives a median 30 seconds after the first). In the measured model (M6),
  against today's 1 × 2 book, day SD is 119 against 177 and the 1% day is −446 against
  −545, with expected P&L unchanged (+0.3 ± 1.1). It is better than today on SD and the
  1% day in **all four** models.
- **What it did not measure.** Expected P&L: no interval excludes zero, in the models or
  the replay. Nothing here is evidence of an edge, and the side-level edge has flipped sign
  between samples.
- **The rule, which this release DOES ship (§A5).** The research's §5.1 is *at most one
  open position per side*. Without it the book is the accidental one: about 30% of days
  hold two same-side spreads, and those chained pairs carry most of the added tail. With
  the rule the research measures SD 96 and a 1% day of −286; without it, 119 and −446.
- **What the caps change in practice.**
  - **Two spreads open at once, on opposite sides** (§A5 forbids two on one side). The
    per-trade-idea attribution that guards chaining (the fourth standing landmine) still
    carries weight: the per-side counts are built on it, and a sequential same-side
    re-entry after a close can still chain to a closed position's strikes.
  - **After a realized loss of more than ~150, the budget refuses the second lot while
    the first is open** (1050 − loss − ~450 < ~450). That is the budget doing its job.
    At 950 the same happened after any loss over ~50, that is, after almost any stop.
  - **A 10-wide ladder-gap spread is never takeable** (475 against its ~950 max loss). A
    5-wide at the 0.50 minimum credit costs 450 and fits.
  - **Two positions in the watchdog at once.** Every per-position path (marks, stops,
    the flatten, the spent-exit pages, the residual recheck) now runs for two positions at
    once for the first time in live. Chained-pair behaviour under the exit path is covered
    by tests; live has not run it.
- **A finding the research states and this release accepts:** both sides can be stopped on
  the same day (14% of M6 days) — the whipsaw case, and the condor's own failure mode. Its
  tail is still the smallest in the table.

**A netted (condor) budget is not built and must not be** (research §4.6: it re-arms full
size after losses and raises the worst day).

**The per-side rule, §5.1, is built in this release (§A5).** A `/status` Book row for it
is the one follow-up owed.

#### A3. The flatten deferral is allowed at two slots

`flatten_deferral = "on"` was refused by the loader above one concurrent position, on the
reasoning that same-side spreads share a barrier event. The caps move above would have
refused the deploy, so the coupling is **re-keyed from the COUNT to the DOLLARS**: the loader
now refuses `"on"` unless the welded invariant holds (`RiskConfig.open_risk_is_welded`, one
predicate the watchdog's runtime condition 6 re-reads rather than copies). What one barrier
event can cost across every deferred position is at most `max_concurrent_positions ×
max_loss_per_position` (2 × 475 = 950), which the invariant holds within `daily_max_loss`
(1050). That is the same 950 the approved one-position configuration could lose at once
(1 × 950). Each position is still decided against
its own barrier (a put and a call at one spot: the nearer flattens, the farther defers).
→ [amendment](../decisions/2026-09-25-deferring-a-far-otm-flatten.md#amendment-more-than-one-position-2026-09-25).
**This is a `src/` change to a reviewed component made after its review**; it has its own
tests (`tests/test_flatten_deferral.py`, `tests/test_watchdog_flatten_deferral.py`).

- the mark and `credit_stop` are still computed and recorded;
- the profit target is still evaluated;
- `unknown` still reports a missing mark, so `blind` and its page are unchanged

**Why.**

- **The week.** −$515, all from eight 2×-credit stops. The stop was right on the 09-21

- **The replay.** +$49–71 a day against the 2× stop over 24 sessions. It holds in both

- **The models.** Better in 3 of 4 path models (momentum −$9/day), and CVaR 1% no worse

- **The pre-registered test.** The per-idea version fails its Holm correction (p 0.04).
- **The grade.** Exploratory at the book level, on one regime.

**Review triggers, fixed now** ([decision](../decisions/2026-09-26-strike-touch-stop.md#reversal-rule-fixed-before-the-first-live-session)):

1. The first five touch stops average worse than −$250 a lot. The replay has −$176 to

2. More than one touch stop per session over ten sessions. The replay has 0.38.
3. Any day worse than −$400. The replay's worst is −$158.

**Interaction:** the flatten deferral's barrier moves to the strike. That is five points

#### A5. One open spread per side (package B)

| Key (all three profiles) | v0.5.1 | v0.5.2 |
|---|---|---|
| `risk.side_balance` | — (new, REQUIRED) | **`true`** |
| `risk.max_side_imbalance` | — (new, REQUIRED) | **1** |

**The rule.**

- An entry is allowed only if afterwards `|puts − calls| <= max_side_imbalance`. At two
  slots that means one put spread and one call spread at most.
- Among the sides that qualify on a tick, the one leaving the book more balanced goes
  first. Ties go in `signal.sides` order, which puts the put first; that is Richard's put
  preference, as the tie-break only.
- The orchestrator chooses and `PreTradeGate` refuses (`side_imbalance_exceeded`).
- `false` is the pre-v0.5.2 entry path exactly.

**Counted per side as the concurrency cap counts in total.** The count is the higher of
the broker's legs (by right) and the ledger's trade ideas, plus working opening orders.
Anything unreadable counts on BOTH sides.

**Also changed:**

- The selector skips candidates the entry pipeline already attempted
  (`EntryPipeline.was_attempted`, read-only), so a repeated put no longer hides a
  qualifying call for the tick.
- The strategy prices every side on every tick: 0.125 ms per tick, against a 0.2 ms
  budget.
- `signal.candidate` carries `other_sides` and a `book` block (the counts, the settings,
  and `choice`: `off` / `first` / `balance` / `refused` / `attempted`).
- `gate.decision` carries the per-side counts.

**Replay at v0.5.2's caps under the strike-touch stop:** day SD 107 → 100 and the worst
day −$258 → −$158. The mean falls $10–19 a day as a main effect, which is not
significant and partly an artifact of the replay imputing the call side.
→ [decision](../decisions/2026-09-26-one-position-per-side.md),
[design](../designs/2026-09-26-multi-spread-side-balance.md).

**Tests:** 37 in `tests/test_side_balance.py`, 10 in `tests/test_other_sides_telemetry.py`,

### 1. The far-OTM flatten is deferred by a calibrated insurance test

`FLATTEN_BEFORE_CLOSE` is insurance against the price triggers and the broker failing
together in the last half hour. It is now withheld on a tick when that insurance is at
least `k` times overpriced:

```
k · P_touch(z) · W  ≤  one tick
z       = ln(spot / barrier) / (max(IV, RV) · √(minutes to close / MINUTES_PER_YEAR))
barrier = short strike ± underlying_buffer_points
P_touch = 0.01 · (z / z99) ^ (−alpha)        for z ≥ z99
```

- **No points or percent anywhere.** 2026-09-17 and 2026-09-23 were both 0.70% out of the
  money and were 5.17σ against 4.55σ. The unit is the standardized distance and the
  threshold is a probability budget.
- **`z99` = 2.33, `alpha` = 2.93** are outputs of `argus.tools.flatten_calibration`
  (read-only): 28 non-event sessions, 15,698 30-minute windows, a Hill estimate of 3.91
  whose day-bootstrap lower bound (2.93) is used, capped at 3. The Gaussian is about 35×
  too thin at 4σ on this path. **`k` = 10 is Richard's policy call.** At `k = 10` on a
  5-wide spread the boundary is z ≥ 5.12.
- **Backtest:** the largest standardized 15:30 → 16:00 move on 25 flatten windows was
  1.93σ (38% of the boundary). **Replay of all 15 recorded flattens defers 2026-09-17
  (z 5.17) and 2026-09-24 (z 6.02) only.**
- **IV is computed by the watchdog itself** from the chain source it already shares,
  never read from `SignalLoop`. RV is `realized_vol` over the watchdog's own trailing
  30-minute spot path.

**It only ever REMOVES the flatten.** Eligibility is all-of, and every failure means
"flatten as today":

- only the clock fired — no credit stop, underlying trigger or profit take beside it;
- nothing sent or working for the position, and **no closing send has failed** (hard
  constraint 1's "spent *or failing*", read literally);
- spot is fresh and the short strike is known from the chain identity, never a leg quote;
- the watchdog has watched spot since 30 minutes before the mark — **a restart after 15:00
  (12:00 on a half-day) disables the deferral for the rest of that day**;
- the last `get_live_orders()` poll and the last scan are within `2 × refresh_interval_s`;
- the watchdog is not degraded;
- the welded size invariant holds (`max_concurrent_positions × max_loss_per_position ≤
  daily_max_loss`) — **the loader refuses `"on"` otherwise, and the watchdog re-reads the
  same predicate every tick** (§A3).

It is re-checked every tick. The credit stop and the underlying trigger stay live to the
close, and it is never applied to a spent position, so the one-shot override and the stub
continuation are untouched. A NaN anywhere refuses (every refusal test is spelled so that
NaN fails it).

`flatten_deferral` is `"off" | "record" | "on"`, and **ships `"on"` in all three profiles.**
`"record"` decides and writes everything but always flattens.

**At two slots the deferral's `k = 10` and calibration are unchanged and still per
position.** Expected losses add whether or not positions are correlated; the concentration
risk (one event touching both) is what the welded dollars bound. The calibration was fitted
and replayed on one-position days.

### 2. The cross is priced by what can fill

`ExitExecutor._cross_limit` priced the market-crossing escalation from the natural
(short ask − long bid) alone, so once `exit_walk_min_steps` went 1 → 3 (v0.4.3) a flatten
opening at 0.05 ended its ladder at 0.20 and then *crossed at 0.10* — two ticks **below**
the price the walk had just failed at.

- **Long leg has a bid:** `max(natural, |last rung| + one ladder step)`. The last rung is
  `walk.final_limit_price`, never `order.limit_price`.
- **Long leg has no bid:** the natural, unchanged, on purpose — with nothing to leg into,
  no price buys a fill, and paying more only overpays.
- Both go through `build.cap_at_width`, now public: the one floor-quantized ceiling.
- `CROSS_UNPRICEABLE` and its retry taxonomy are unchanged.

This corrects a premise of the 2026-09-17 record ("a limit walking the grid cannot skip a
resting offer") — true for a resting *complex* offer, not for a natural derived from *leg*
quotes. The correction is written into the live, sandbox and dev `trigger_source` strings.

### 3. `walked_away_at` reports the rung the walk stopped at

`watchdog.crossed` and its page recorded the walk's **opening** limit under
`walked_away_at`, so 09-24 read as a normal 0.05 → 0.10 escalation for a walk that ended
at −0.20. One `_crossed_detail` builder now feeds both, with `walked_away_at` =
`walk.final_limit_price` plus `opening_limit`, `walk_steps`, `limit_basis`,
`crossing_debit`, `short_ask` and `long_bid` (nothing decides on the last four).

**Reader rule.** A `watchdog.crossed` row carrying `opening_limit` has the corrected
number. A row without it carries the opening limit under that name and is right only where
`exit_finished.walk.steps = 0`. For older rows, compute the deficit from
`watchdog.exit_started.walk_away_price`, joined on `external_identifier`. Rows already
written are not rewritten.

### 4. The resting cross is a tracked close

A `crossed` exit leaves a working order. It is now registered with the residual recheck as
a `working_expected` **CLOSE** carrying its limit:

- it pages only past `unmanaged_order_max_s`, and is **never cancelled**
  (`cancel_licence` refuses a CLOSE);
- the spent-exit page and `/status` name it — *"CLOSE WORKING at the broker … check the
  broker BEFORE sending anything by hand"* — so a manual close cannot stack on it (C5's
  double close). `docs/go-live-findings.md`'s manual-close SOP says the same;
- when it fills it resolves at Tier 2 (`order.residual_resolved`, `sighting: filled`),
  **never** as `order.residual_filled`, whose meaning is "an abandoned order filled and
  the position is real" — the wrong message to someone just stopped out.

### 5. "Daily loss budget exhausted" no longer pages on a ladder gap

The exhaustion page means the day's own losses have used the budget, so closing
everything would not bring back room for one lot. On 2026-09-24 at 14:25 ET it paged on a
put 7680/7670 that was 10 wide only because a stale 7675 quote pushed the long leg out to
`max_width` for one tick: its lot cost 922.5 against 866.8 flat, while a 5-wide lot of
about 450 still fit almost twice over. Entries had not ended, and the page spent the
day's only notice.

The flat check now asks about **one ordinary lot**: the smaller of the proposal's own
per-lot max loss and `(signal.width − signal.min_credit) × 100` (450 in live), measured
per lot rather than at the proposal's full contract count. **The refusal is unchanged; only
the page moved.** No config key. Pinned by
`test_2026_09_24_a_ladder_gap_candidate_does_not_spend_the_days_page`
(`tests/test_loss_budget.py`).

### 6. Finding 3 is closed by measurement

A quiet far-OTM quote reads as stale (DXLink "publishes events only as they occur"). But
99.6% of 21,423 stale-leg ticks came while the mark was below 25% of the credit stop, all
six live credit stops fired on fresh quotes, and for the flatten the deferral removes the
case. **Nothing was built.** Three reopen triggers are recorded in the decision: a stop
delayed or unfilled while a leg was stale; stale ticks at ≥ 50% of the stop above 1% of all
stale ticks over five sessions; or the size rails rising. `market_data_max_age_s` stays
untouched and must not be raised to "fix" it.

### Reviewed before this release

A code review the same day found seven defects and one open question in the build; **all
eight are fixed** (12 mutations of the fixes, 12 caught by tests). Every fix is in the safe
direction: it defers less, records less or pages less. The largest: the outcome row was
almost never written in production, because it waited for a pass at or after 16:00:00
while the scheduler stops the session *at* the close — `PositionWatchdog.stop()` writes it
now; and the documented intraday rollback was false until a restart-after-15:00 condition
was added. The table is in the decision.

## Rules to carry

- **A flatten threshold is never a distance in points or percent.**
- **`z99` and `alpha` are calibration outputs — rerun the tool, never hand-tune.** `k` is
  policy: raising it is the safe direction and needs no analysis; lowering it needs a
  calibration rerun and a decision record. Either way it is a config-only release, **never
  between 15:00 and 16:00 with a position held.**
- **Never rest the intraday rollback on `realized_vol`'s refusal alone.** It accepts 80% of
  its window and re-arms ~24 minutes after any restart.
- **Never apply the deferral to a spent position, and never let it remove anything but the
  flatten** (AST-pinned). **It reads no leg price** (AST-pinned).
- **A cross above the ladder is only for a book where a leg-in exists.**
- **The resting cross is a registered CLOSE: paged, never cancelled.**

### Recalibration triggers

Rerun `tools.flatten_calibration` and cut a config-only release when any of these happens:
a `flatten_deferral_outcome` records `touched: true`; any non-event 30-minute window exceeds
5.35σ; the monthly re-fit moves α or z₉₉ by more than their bootstrap interval; or the data
grows by 50%.

## Why it's safe

- **The deferral only removes an order.** No new send path, and the underlying trigger
  runs to the close. (The release as a whole is not a removal: the stop moves to the
  strike, which holds a losing spread longer before closing it, §A4; and the caps allow a
  second position, on the other side, §A5.)
- **Every uncertainty flattens.** A stale poll, a stale scan, a blind backstop, a degraded
  watchdog, a NaN, an unreadable IV or a restart inside the last half hour all mean "flatten
  as today".
- **The loader bounds the deferral by dollars.** It refuses `"on"` unless two deferred
  positions could not together lose more than the day's budget.
- **The worst realistic case is bounded and priced.** A deferred spread that goes to the
  money in the last 30 minutes is held to settlement — the same outcome as a flatten that
  found no bid, which is what happened on two of the five recorded flattens. The tail
  probability is calibrated on 15,698 windows with the *heavier* of two tail estimates, and
  the largest observed move was 38% of the boundary.

## What this release does NOT do

- **It does not switch the profit target on** (§A1), and does not change the take itself.
- **It does not change `credit_multiple`'s role**: the trigger is switched off, not
  re-tuned, and 3.0 is only the value a rollback returns to.
- **It does not raise open risk.** `2 × 475 = 950` is what `1 × 950` authorized. The
  day's loss cap does rise, 950 → 1050, to give the second slot one ordinary stop of
  slack (§A2).
- **It does not build the re-entry**, and does not change `signal.entry_latest`.
- **It does not net the loss budget** for a put-and-call pair.

- **The zero-mid refusal is not touched.** It is moot for every deferred position;
  09-22-shape flattens (z 3.33) still meet it and send nothing. That is the honest residual.
- **No short-leg-only close** — it would break the single-legging non-negotiable.
- **The ladder (`exit_walk_min_steps = 3`), `_await_settled`, the one-attempt rule,
  `_EXIT_SEND_ATTEMPTS`, the size rails and `market_data_max_age_s`** are all unchanged.
- **The loss-budget refusal is unchanged** — only which refusals *page*.
- **It does not change the stub close** as v0.5.1 shipped it (armed; dormant at one lot).

### Rollback

Per behaviour, each a one-line config edit and a restart (no image change), **never with a
position open**:

- the strike-touch stop: `credit_trigger_enabled = true`, `underlying_buffer_points = 5.0`;
- the side rule: `side_balance = false`;
- flatten deferral: `flatten_deferral = "off"` (`"record"` keeps every record and always
  flattens);
- caps: `max_concurrent_positions = 1`, `max_loss_per_position = 950.0`,
  `max_contracts_per_position = 2`, `daily_max_loss = 950.00`. **Keep the four
  together**: a partial set can break the welded invariant (and, with the deferral on,
  the loader refuses the config), and leaving 1050 behind would keep a larger day on a
  one-slot book.

Full rollback: `/opt/argus/scripts/argus-deploy.sh v0.5.1` — image and config roll back
together (the tag carries both, and the v0.5.1 image has no such keys). **Never with a live
position open.** If the droplet was never moved to v0.5.1, roll back to the tag it was
actually running.
